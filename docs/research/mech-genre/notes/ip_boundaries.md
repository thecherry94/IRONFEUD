# IP boundaries for an original MechWarrior/BattleTech-inspired mech game

> **Not legal advice.** These are awareness notes compiled from public sources: court records as reported by secondary sources, trademark databases, law-firm commentary and fan wikis (Sarna). Jurisdictions differ. The developer is in Germany, so German/EU law applies to him directly, but a Steam/itch release is worldwide and US law (where the rights holders and most of the case law are) matters in practice. Before any commercial release, a short consultation with a German IP lawyer (Urheber- und Markenrecht) would be cheap insurance.
>
> Source quality: many lawsuit details come from Sarna.net (the BattleTech fan wiki). It cites court filings, but it is not a primary source. Where I could not reach the opinion text I say so.

## 1. Which terms are trademarks, who owns them, and is "mech" free?

### Takeaway
The franchise word marks (BATTLETECH, MECHWARRIOR, BATTLEMECH) are registered US trademarks owned by **The Topps Company**. **Microsoft** holds the video-game rights and at least one MechWarrior-related mark, and Catalyst Game Labs and Piranha Games are licensees. The bare word "mech" or "mecha" is a genre term. I found no registration of it and no enforcement against it. Franchise compounds and coined terms ('Mech, BattleMech, OmniMech, MechWarrior) are where the risk sits, along with franchise-specific proper nouns (Inner Sphere, named Clans).

### Cited Findings
- BATTLETECH is a US word mark owned by The Topps Company: Reg. No. **1403297** (serial 73577249, registered 1986, Class 028 board-game equipment), listed as registered and renewed. A second registration, **1730183** (filed 1990, paper goods and entertainment services), was listed as renewed in May 2023. Source: search results summarising [TrademarkElite BATTLETECH 73577249](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/73577249/BATTLETECH) and [TrademarkElite BATTLETECH 74084214](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/74084214/BATTLETECH). I did not open these record pages directly.
- BATTLEMECH, serial **73626301**, filed 22 Oct 1986, is owned by Topps. Its current status is unconfirmed, and Justia's category filter for "battlemech" shows one Topps mark in "Toys and sporting goods" — [Justia October 1986 filings](https://trademark.justia.com/category/toys-and-sporting-goods/1986/october/22); [Justia search "battlemech"](https://trademarks.justia.com/search?q=battlemech), read via browser: owner filter "THE TOPPS COMPANY, INC. (1)".
- A Justia search for "mechwarrior" returns 17+ marks. Owners: The Topps Company (11 + 3 + 1 under name variants), WizKids Inc./LLC (6, the earlier Topps subsidiary) and **Microsoft Corporation (1)**. Classes include electrical/scientific apparatus (the software class), entertainment, printed matter and toys. The record list itself did not render — [Justia search "mechwarrior"](https://trademarks.justia.com/search?q=mechwarrior).
- In 2011 Topps filed BATTLETECH (serial 85289609, Class 9 films and downloadable media; prior regs 1403297, 1563309, 1730183) and MECHWARRIOR (serial 85318601, Class 41 entertainment services; prior reg 1404241) — [Cardboard Connection](https://www.cardboardconnection.com/topps-trademark-filings-also-hint-at-battletech-and-mechwarrior-movies/amp).
- The franchise is owned by Topps and produced under licence by Catalyst Game Labs. Microsoft (Xbox Game Studios / Microsoft Gaming) holds the video-game side — [Wikipedia: BattleTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech); [Sarna: BattleTech](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/BattleTech).
- When FASA's IP went to WizKids in 2001, the computer-game licence stayed with Microsoft. WizKids was bought by Topps in 2003, and Topps kept BattleTech after shutting WizKids down in 2008 — summarised from [Sarna: BattleTech](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/BattleTech) and [ICv2 on Catalyst's licence extension](https://icv2.com/articles/games/view/24979/catalyst-keeps-shadowrun).
- In 2007, FASA founder Jordan Weisman's company licensed the MechWarrior, Shadowrun and Crimson Skies electronic rights back from Microsoft — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/50247/fasa-founder-regains-rights-to).

### Inferences
- **Avoid** the following as names, titles, store tags or in-game terms: *BattleTech, MechWarrior, BattleMech, 'Mech* (apostrophe form), *OmniMech, MechCommander, MechAssault, Inner Sphere, Periphery* (as a polity name), *Clan Wolf/Jade Falcon/Smoke Jaguar* and the other named Clans, *Star League, ComStar, Great Houses* (Davion, Steiner, Kurita, Liao, Marik), *Elemental* (battle armour), *Trial of Possession, bondsman, freebirth/trueborn*. Only some of these are registered trademarks. The rest are franchise expression (names plus lore), and using them together signals derivation. The bar for trademark infringement is likelihood of confusion, but the project rule ("no names") is stricter, and that is the right call.
- **"mech", "mecha", "walker", "frame", "exosuit"** are generic genre words, used across many unrelated titles (Hawken, Titanfall, Armored Core, Front Mission, Lancer). Calling units "mechs" in text is fine. Avoid making "Mech" part of a coined compound that echoes the franchise (e.g. "IronMech", "BattleFrame-Mech"), and don't use the apostrophe spelling "'Mech", which is a BattleTech typographic signature.
- A **generic word** such as "Clan" is not protectable on its own. "The Clans" as a set of genetically engineered warrior castes descended from an exiled army is franchise expression. The safe test: would a fan read the term in context and think "that's the BattleTech X"? If yes, rename.
- The title **IRONFEUD** looks unproblematic against these marks. A proper clearance search on USPTO, EUIPO and DPMA (DPMAregister) before release is cheap and worth it. Search for conflicting game titles in Class 9/41, not franchise marks.

### Gaps
- I could not open USPTO TSDR or EUIPO eSearch records directly (Justia/TrademarkElite returned 403 or Cloudflare; TSDR was not attempted within the tool budget). So the current status of BATTLEMECH, any OMNIMECH filing, Microsoft's specific mark(s) and EU registrations are **unverified**. Check TSDR for serial 73626301 and search EUIPO for "MechWarrior" and "BattleTech".
- No source found on whether "Inner Sphere" or "Clan" are registered as marks. They are most likely protected only as copyrighted lore context, not as trademarks.
- I found no source on any registration or enforcement of the bare word "mech". The genericness claim above is inference from widespread use.

## 2. Copyright vs. game mechanics (US, EU, Germany)

### Takeaway
On both sides of the Atlantic, **rules, systems and mechanics are unprotectable ideas or methods**. What is protected is the **concrete expression**: art, models, audio, text, specific UI look, code, and names together with lore. Heat management, torso twist, hardpoints, sectioned armour, weight classes, salvage and a mechlab are all free to use as concepts. The danger zone is copying the *look* of the franchise's armour diagram, record sheet, HUD or specific numeric tables wholesale, so that the "total concept and feel" or the specific expressive choices match. *Tetris v. Xio* shows that a clone which copies every visual expression of free mechanics still loses.

### Cited Findings
- **US Copyright Office (FL-108, "Games"):** "Copyright does not protect the idea for a game, its name or title, or the method or methods for playing it." The text describing the rules, and artwork, can be protected if sufficiently expressive — [archived FL-108 (2004)](https://webharvest.gov/peth04/20041015023438/http://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl108.html); [Copyright Office TX games guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html).
- ***Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive*, 863 F. Supp. 2d 394 (D.N.J. 2012):** Judge Wolfson held there is no copyright in game rules or mechanics, which are ideas. Tetris was still protected in the way it chose to express them, particularly the look and feel: piece shapes, colours, board dimensions, the display of the next piece, and so on. Merger did not apply because Xio could have expressed the same mechanics differently. Xio admitted copying, and its scènes-à-faire and functionality defences failed — [Loeb & Loeb summary](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2012/06/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc); [FBA IP Insight PDF](https://www.fedbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/ipinsight-octnov2012-pdf-1.pdf). Commentators say the decisive fight in clone cases is the **level of abstraction** at which mechanics are described — [Cardozo AELJ](https://cardozoaelj.com/?p=2943).
- ***Spry Fox v. Lolapps/6waves* (W.D. Wash., No. 2:12-cv-00147-RAJ, 2012):** The copyright claim over *Yeti Town* cloning *Triple Town* survived a motion to dismiss on substantial similarity. Eric Goldman notes the order "guts the copyright eligibility of some key elements" of the gameplay and UI. He also notes that the title is not copyrightable, but the choice of "Yeti Town" was relevant to similarity. The case settled, with Spry Fox taking ownership of the Yeti Town IP — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spry_Fox,_LLC_v._Lolapps,_Inc.); [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=10653); [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/triple-town-cloning-suit-settled/).
- ***Data East v. Epyx*, 862 F.2d 204 (9th Cir. 1988)** (karate games): Similarities that flow necessarily from the idea of a karate game are **scènes à faire** and must be excluded. Protection was limited to the author's own contribution (e.g. scoreboard and background), and the infringement finding was reversed — [Patent Arcade](https://www.patentarcade.com/?p=1186); [Wikipedia: Analytic dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_dissection).
- ***Lotus v. Borland*, 49 F.3d 807 (1st Cir. 1995)**, aff'd by an equally divided Court, 516 U.S. 233 (1996): A menu command hierarchy is an uncopyrightable **"method of operation"** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Development_Corp._v._Borland_International,_Inc.). This is a 1st Circuit ruling, persuasive elsewhere.
- **An ABA Landslide article** argues that courts do not consistently separate uncopyrightable rules from expression in video-game cases, so outcomes are less predictable than the doctrine suggests — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/intellectual_property_law/resources/landslide/archive/why-videogame-rules-are-not-expression-protected-copyright-law/).
- **EU: *SAS Institute v World Programming*, C-406/10 (CJEU, 2 May 2012):** Neither the functionality of a computer program, nor its programming language, nor its data file formats are protected under the Software Directive. Replicating functionality without copying code or other expression did not infringe. Such elements might still be protected under the InfoSoc Directive if they are the author's own intellectual creation — [SCL report](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/); [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2012/05/07/european-court-of-justice-rules-on-copyright-status-of-computer-programming-languages-and-functionality/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc_v_World_Programming_Ltd).
- **Germany:** The game idea, the abstract concept, the theme and the genre are not protected (§ 2 UrhG). Only the concrete form is protected. For code, § 69a(2) sentence 2 UrhG excludes the ideas and principles underlying a program element. LG Mannheim (7 O 240/07) held a dice game's idea unprotectable, though its written instructions could be protected. The BGH "Action Replay II" ruling (I ZR 157/21, 31.07.2025) is cited as confirming the narrow scope of program protection — [Ferner Alsdorf: Recht der Entwicklung von Computerspielen](https://www.ferner-alsdorf.de/recht-der-entwicklung-von-computerspielen/); [Ferner Alsdorf: Spielidee](https://www.ferner-alsdorf.de/schutzfaehigkeit-von-spielidee-oder-schriftlich-niedergelegter-spielanleitung/). The German game designers' guild (Spieleautorenzunft) argues for a minority position that worked-out rules can be protected, citing OLG Köln GRUR-RR 2013, 1. This is contested — [SAZ Zeichen Nr. 5 (PDF)](https://www.spieleautorenzunft.de/files/public/SAZ-Zeichen-DE/SAZ-Zeichen%20Nr%205%20-%20Spiele%20und%20Urheberrechtsschutz.pdf).

### Inferences (applied to IRONFEUD's feature list)
- **Free as ideas:** heat build-up and dissipation, overheat shutdown, torso twist independent of legs, per-section armour with internal structure and criticals, weight classes by tonnage, hardpoints and slots, a mechlab, a mercenary company with contracts, salvage and repair, jump jets, missile lock and ECM, convergence. These are all methods of play.
- **Not free, so build your own expression:**
  - The **paper-doll armour diagram** in BattleTech record-sheet style (the specific dot/circle armour bubbles, section layout and labels).
  - The exact **critical-slot table** structure (e.g. 12 slots per torso with "roll 2d6" location tables).
  - Lifting **numeric tables** wholesale (weapon damage/heat/range brackets copied 1:1 from TechManuals). Single numbers are not protectable, but a large compiled table copied verbatim is a risk (compilation) and makes derivation obvious.
  - **HUD layouts** that replicate a specific MechWarrior game's screen arrangement and visual style.
- *Tetris v. Xio* is the warning case. Each element was "just a mechanic", but copying all their visual expressions together made it infringing. *Data East* is the comfort case: similarities that are genre necessities (a torso-twist indicator, a heat bar, a radar) are scènes à faire.
- IRONFEUD's own code is original C#, and its systems (procedural gait, 120 Hz sim, its own heat model with 0..1+ heat) are its own implementations. Even SAS v WPL-style functional emulation would be lawful in the EU. This project is far from it anyway.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full opinion texts (Tetris, Data East, Spry Fox, SAS). Holdings come from law-firm and academic summaries.
- The current (post-2004) wording of the Copyright Office games factsheet was not fetched. FL-108 at copyright.gov returned 404, so the archived 2004 version is cited.
- Not researched: EU/German **design law** (unregistered Community design, 3 years from first disclosure in the EU; registered designs), which could protect a specific mech appearance separately from copyright. German "Leistungsschutz"/UWG § 4 Nr. 3 (unfair imitation, *ergänzender wettbewerbsrechtlicher Leistungsschutz*), which can catch slavish imitation of a product even without copyright. Both are worth asking a German lawyer about if a mech design ends up close to a franchise design.

## 3. The "Unseen" history and what made designs infringing

### Takeaway
BattleTech's own history shows two lessons. (1) **Chain of title**: FASA licensed anime mecha art through an importer (TCI) whose rights were disputed, and that cost it a dozen iconic designs from 1996 until 2018. (2) **Substantial similarity of visual design is the test, and it is demanding**. In *FASA v. Playmates* (1996), the court found FASA's original BattleMech designs copyrightable. The court found that an Exo-Squad toy "bore a strong resemblance" to the Mad Cat/Timber Wolf, but it was still **not substantially similar** and did not infringe. The 2017 Harmony Gold suit over redesigned ("Reseen") Unseen mechs ended in dismissal and settlement, not a ruling on similarity.

### Cited Findings
- **Origins (1984):** FASA licensed mecha art via Twentieth Century Imports (TCI). The designs came from *Super Dimension Fortress Macross*: VF-1 Valkyrie variants → Wasp, Stinger, Valkyrie, Phoenix Hawk, Crusader; Destroids → Rifleman, Archer, Warhammer, Longbow; Glaug → Marauder. Others came from *Crusher Joe* (Locust and others) and *Fang of the Sun Dougram* (Shadow Hawk, Griffin, Scorpion, Wolverine, Thunderbolt, Goliath, BattleMaster) — [Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen).
- **FASA v. Playmates (N.D. Ill., No. 93 C 2445, Judge Castillo):**
  - FASA sued over Playmates' *Exo-Squad* toys (copyright, trademark, unfair competition) concerning seven *original* FASA designs (Timber Wolf/Mad Cat, Dire Wolf, etc.) and the Elemental armour.
  - FASA III (22 Jan 1996): the seven BattleMech designs were protected by copyright, but the **Elemental was not**.
  - **No infringement**: the Heavy Attack E-Frame "bore a strong resemblance" to the Mad Cat, but the E-Frames were "not substantially similar", and there was no likelihood of confusion.
  - On fees after remand, Castillo said Playmates' line was "just barely outside the reach" of copyright and trademark law.
  - The 7th Circuit (6 Mar 1997) vacated and remanded on the fee standard.
  - Sources: [Sarna: Unseen lawsuits](https://server6.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen_lawsuits); earlier opinions are listed at 869 F. Supp. 1334 (1994) and 892 F. Supp. 1061 (1995) — [Studicata](https://studicata.com/case-briefs/case/fasa-corp-v-playmates-toys-inc); [Quimbee](https://quimbee.com/cases/fasa-corp-v-playmates-toys-inc).
- **Harmony Gold USA v. FASA (refiled 1995, transferred to N.D. Ill.):**
  - Harmony Gold had sent cease-and-desist letters since January 1985 over the Macross-derived designs.
  - On 12 June 1996, Judge Anderson rejected FASA's argument that Harmony Gold lacked standing (FASA had argued HG's 1984 Tatsunoko licence excluded model kits).
  - The court **never ruled** on whether TCI could license the designs.
  - The settlement took effect 19 Dec 1996 under an NDA. FASA stopped using "the visual design images of the twelve (12) Battlemechs", agreed to a permanent injunction, and Weisman agreed not to contest HG's worldwide (ex-Japan) Robotech merchandising rights. These terms were quoted later in the 2017 complaint.
  - Sources: [Sarna: Unseen lawsuits](https://server6.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen_lawsuits); [Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen) (which describes the settlement as 1997 and says molds were destroyed; the dates conflict between the two Sarna pages).
- A 2002/2003 Tokyo District Court ruling found that **Studio Nue** owns the copyright in the Macross art and Tatsunoko the production/distribution rights. This casts doubt on the scope of Harmony Gold's rights — [Sarna: Unseen lawsuits](https://server6.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen_lawsuits).
- **2009:** Catalyst briefly announced the Unseen's return (24 June) and then reverted (10 Aug). On 3 Sep 2009, Harmony Gold sent IGN a C&D over the Warhammer in the MechWarrior reboot trailer (it resembles the Destroid Tomahawk) — [Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen).
- **2015 "Reseen":** Catalyst retconned the Unseen with new art for every design. Harebrained's BATTLETECH (2018) and MWO used redesigned versions — [Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen).
- **Harmony Gold v. Harebrained Schemes, Piranha Games, IMR/Catalyst, Weisman (complaint dated 1 Mar 2017):**
  - Claims: copyright infringement, plus breach of the 1996 settlement (against Weisman and Harebrained).
  - Designs at issue: the redesigned Warhammer, Marauder, Archer, Rifleman; also Atlas, Shadow Hawk and Locust in HBS's game.
  - Catalyst defaulted (entered 28 Jun 2017).
  - The case against Harebrained (Atlas, Shadow Hawk, Locust) was dismissed with prejudice on 9 Apr 2018.
  - Piranha led the defence and negotiated an undisclosed settlement, reported as with prejudice around 20 June 2018, that lets all three keep using the redesigned Unseen. It reportedly overturned the default against Catalyst. Piranha reportedly raised doubts about HG's standing over the Unseen.
  - Harebrained reintroduced the designs in the 2019 *Heavy Metal* expansion.
  - Sources: [Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen); [Sarna: Unseen lawsuits](https://server6.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen_lawsuits); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/battletech-and-mechwarrior-online-facing-legal-problems/ze4fd); [GameSpark (JP), 2018-06-29](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2018/06/29/81968.html); [GameSpark (JP), 2018-04-18](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2018/04/18/80137.html); [GameStar (DE)](https://www.gamestar.de/artikel/battletech-und-mechwarrior-5-macher-werden-wegen-mech-designs-verklagt,3317497.html).
- The settlement terms are not public. Statements that "all parties can continue using the designs" come from reports and forums, not the court record — [Sarna News](https://www.sarna.net/news/?p=5451); [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/posts/7752138/).

### Inferences
- **What the courts actually tested** was visual design, not mechanics: silhouette, proportions, distinctive part shapes and their combination. *FASA v. Playmates* shows that a design "strongly resembling" another can still fall short of substantial similarity. Do not treat that as a safe harbour, though: it was a costly multi-year fight, and the outcome was "just barely" in the defendant's favour.
- The Harmony Gold saga shows that **even a 30-year-old, redesigned derivative** draws lawsuits when the rights holder is litigious. The cost of defending is the real risk for a hobby developer, whatever the merits. A design anyone can name as "that's basically a Warhammer/Atlas/Timber Wolf" invites a C&D.
- The Elemental finding (not protected) suggests that very generic forms, such as a humanoid armoured suit, get thin or no protection. The more distinctive the design, the more protection it gets.
- Lesson for asset sourcing: **chain of title** matters. Only use assets (models, sounds, voice) you made yourself, commissioned with a written assignment, or took under a clear licence (CC0/CC-BY with records). Keep provenance notes. This applies directly to IRONFEUD's TTS (Kokoro) and Stable Audio Open assets: check those models' output licence terms.

### Gaps
- No primary opinion text was retrieved for FASA III or the HG v. FASA standing ruling. The quotes come via Sarna.
- No official public text of the 2018 settlement exists. The Catalyst statement page (bg.battletech.com) failed DNS resolution.
- The Polygon/Kotaku coverage of the 2017 suit was not retrieved. Gamepressure, GameSpark and GameStar were used instead.

## 4. Other relevant cases and fan projects

### Takeaway
Microsoft tolerates and sometimes explicitly licenses **non-commercial** fan works under its revocable Game Content Usage Rules. MechWarrior: Living Legends was formally cleared. Fan software such as MegaMek exists unaffiliated, under the GPL. None of this helps an original game that wants to sell: the Rules forbid monetisation, and IRONFEUD uses no franchise content anyway. I found **no documented case of an original mech indie game taken down for genre resemblance**. The litigation in this space is about specific visual designs.

### Cited Findings
- **Microsoft Game Content Usage Rules** (last updated Jan 2015):
  - Fans may use Microsoft game content (footage, screenshots, music) to make "Items", but may not sell them or earn compensation. Exceptions: YouTube/Twitch ad revenue, optional donations, contests.
  - Microsoft logos may not be used in an Item's own logos, and titles must not imply official endorsement.
  - A notice with a link to the Rules is required: game © Microsoft, not endorsed by Microsoft.
  - "We can't give you permission to use games from other publishers."
  - "We can revoke this limited-use license at any time and for any reason without liability to you."
  - Distributing an Item grants Microsoft a licence to it.
  - Source: [Microsoft/Xbox Game Content Usage Rules](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/developers/rules).
- **MechWarrior: Living Legends** (Crysis mod) was reportedly given legal clearance by Microsoft to use BattleTech assets, making it one of the few Microsoft-sanctioned mods — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/49121/mechwarrior-crysis-mod-given-legal). Development stopped in 2013 after talks with Piranha. PGI president Russ Bullock said "there was no combative relationship ... nor was there a cease and desist letter given". A Steam user post claims a C&D (unverified) — [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/news/death-from-above-mechwarrior-living-legends-ceases-development/); [Sarna: MWLL](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/MWLL). The community released v0.8.0 in October 2016 with the founders' permission — [Sarna: MWLL](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/MWLL).
- Microsoft approved a **free release of MechWarrior 4** (MekTek) after delays — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/free-release-of-mechwarrior-4-approved/1100-6259454/); [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/63136/mechwarrior-4-free-release-held).
- **MegaMek/MekHQ** are GPLv2/v3 open-source tabletop simulators, described as "not licensed by or affiliated with" Topps or Catalyst. The README points users to Catalyst rulebooks — [MegaMek GitHub](https://github.com/MegaMek); [EverybodyWiki: MegaMek](https://en.everybodywiki.com/MegaMek); [Sarna: MekHQ](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/MekHQ).
- **Harmony Gold** has also litigated outside BattleTech: for example *Harmony Gold v. Hasbro* (Transformers toys, dismissed 2013) — [Anime Herald](https://www.animeherald.com/2013/10/18/harmony-gold-v-hasbro-transformers-case-dismissed/).

### Inferences
- Fan projects survive on non-commercial status and the rights holder's goodwill. Their tolerance says nothing about a commercial clone. IRONFEUD's "original IP only" rule moves it out of that category entirely, which is the correct posture for a possible public release.
- Do **not** ship anything derived from MegaMek code or data (GPL, and its data files encode franchise content), from MWO/MW5/HBS assets, or from fan-wiki stat tables.

### Gaps
- No reliable source was found on **Hawken** facing IP claims, or on any indie mech game receiving a C&D or Steam DMCA for resembling BattleTech. Searches returned only the Unseen material. This is consistent with "genre resemblance alone doesn't trigger action", but absence of evidence is not proof.
- No official Catalyst Game Labs fan-content policy was located. Catalyst does have one for its tabletop lines; check battletech.com.

## 5. Practical guidelines for original mech design (names, silhouettes, sound, UI, weapons)

### Takeaway
Keep the *systems*, and change the *surface*: own silhouettes (judged at thumbnail size), own naming scheme, own sound and voice, own HUD visual language. Generic weapon categories (laser, autocannon, missiles, machine gun, flamer, railgun) are free. Franchise-coined labels and abbreviations used as a set (PPC, LRM/SRM, Gauss Rifle, "Streak", "Artemis", "Clan ER") are not illegal one by one, but together they read as "BattleTech serial numbers filed off". IRONFEUD's current naming (e.g. "particle cannon", "strike rack", "arc missiles", "siege cannon", Hauberk/Sicklefoot) already follows this.

### Cited Findings
- Courts compare **visual design** of mechs on substantial similarity. A "strong resemblance" was not enough in *FASA v. Playmates*, but it produced years of litigation — [Sarna: Unseen lawsuits](https://server6.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen_lawsuits).
- Harmony Gold's 2009 C&D targeted a *single* redesigned mech in a trailer (the Warhammer, resembling the Destroid Tomahawk) — [Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen). One recognisable silhouette is enough to draw a letter.
- Titles are not copyrightable, but a title that echoes the original is evidence of copying in a similarity analysis ("Yeti Town" vs "Triple Town") — [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=10653).
- A **cockpit warning voice** is a real-world aviation convention. Pilots call female aural warning voices "Bitching Betty", e.g. the F/A-18 voice by Leslie Shook (retired 2016) and Kim Crow's F-15 voice — [Wikipedia: Voice warning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_warning_system); [Marine Corps Times](https://www.marinecorpstimes.com/news/your-marine-corps/2016/03/14/woman-behind-the-f-a-18-s-bitchin-betty-voice-retires/). The concept is generic. Specific recorded lines and their exact wording and delivery in MechWarrior games are copyrighted sound recordings and scripts.
- Rules and mechanics are free, and expression is protected (US FL-108; Tetris v. Xio; Germany § 2 UrhG / § 69a(2) UrhG) — sources in section 2.

### Inferences (actionable checklist, not legally sourced line-by-line)
**Silhouette and model design**
- Run a **"name that mech" test** on every chassis. Show a 64-px black silhouette to someone who knows the genre. If they name a franchise mech (Atlas skull head, Timber Wolf/Mad Cat bird-leg torso with shoulder missile boxes and arm pods, Warhammer forearm cannons with shoulder searchlight, Marauder glaug-shaped body, Catapult missile-ear chicken walker, Urbanmech trash-can, Raven/Jenner), change it.
- Avoid **signature combinations** more than single features. Digitigrade legs, shoulder missile racks and a cockpit canopy are each genre-common. Digitigrade legs + forward-swept torso + boxy shoulder racks + split arm pods is the Timber Wolf. Watch Sicklefoot (digitigrade light) and any heavy with shoulder racks against this test.
- **Heads and cockpits** are the most recognisable feature: skull-face (Atlas), glaug body-pod (Marauder), bubble canopy over a chin gun (Locust/Stinger). IRONFEUD's tiny recessed cockpit is a distinctive choice. Keep it.
- Document your design process (sketches, references used, dated commits). In a dispute, independent creation is a defence, and records show it.

**Names**
- Chassis: your own naming scheme. Hauberk and Sicklefoot (armour and blade terms) are good, coherent and not franchise names. Check each new name against Sarna's 'Mech list. Many common animal and myth names (Atlas, Warhammer, Hunchback, Catapult, Jenner, Raven, Timber Wolf, Dire Wolf, Mad Cat) are BattleTech chassis. One overlapping common word is not infringing, but avoid them as a habit.
- Factions, places and lore: invent everything. No Great Houses, Clans, ComStar-like priestly comm monopoly, Star League, Inner Sphere/Periphery. The "neo-feudal houses + mercenaries" genre premise is free. Copying the specific map, faction set and history is not.

**Weapons**
- Clearly generic (real-world or SF-generic): laser (pulse/beam), autocannon, machine gun, flamethrower, rocket pod, missile, railgun, coilgun, particle beam/cannon, mortar, artillery, AMS/point defense, ECM, jump jets, heat sinks, "Gauss" as a physics term.
- Franchise-flavoured, avoid as **labels**: "PPC" (Particle Projector Cannon), "LRM-20/SRM-6" naming with tube-count suffixes, "Gauss Rifle" as a named item, "Streak SRM", "Artemis IV", "LB 10-X", "ER/Clan" prefixes, "MASC", "TSM", "CASE", "Endo Steel/Ferro-Fibrous", "XL engine", "TAG/NARC". Individually these are short phrases (no copyright) and most are not registered marks. As a **set** they are the most recognisable franchise vocabulary. Current IRONFEUD terms (particle cannon, strike rack, arc missiles, designator marks, siege cannon) are good.
- Don't copy weapon stat tables (damage/heat/range brackets/tonnage/slots) from TechManuals or MW games. Tune your own from your sim, which IRONFEUD does already.

**Sound and voice**
- Synthesised or self-recorded only. Write your own warning-voice lines (e.g. "Heat critical", "Coolant flush", "Shutdown imminent" are fine as functional phrases). Don't reproduce signature line sequences or the delivery of MechWarrior's "Betty" ("Reactor online. Sensors online. Weapons online. All systems nominal." is the famous one; avoid that sequence and its cadence).
- Music: no soundalikes of the MechWarrior 2/4 themes.

**UI**
- Torso-twist indicator, heat bar, paper-doll damage display, radar and target info are functional conventions (scènes à faire). Draw them in your own visual language. Don't mimic a specific MW game's layout or the BattleTech record-sheet armour-dot style.

**Release hygiene (if published)**
- Trademark clearance of the game title (USPTO, EUIPO, DPMA). No franchise names in store tags, description or marketing ("MechWarrior-like" in a store description is nominative or comparative use and legally arguable, but invites trouble; "inspired by classic mech sims" is safer).
- Keep an asset provenance log, including AI-generated audio model licences.
- A German lawyer consult before a commercial launch: copyright, design law (Geschmacksmuster), UWG imitation protection, and trademarks.

### Gaps
- No source was found that rules on short weapon names or abbreviations (PPC, LRM, Gauss rifle) in this franchise. The guidance is inference from the general rule that names and short phrases are not copyrightable plus trademark-confusion logic. No evidence was found that any of these are registered marks.
- No legal source was found on soundalike voice lines in games. US right-of-publicity and EU voice-rights issues apply only when imitating a specific real performer.
