# Sensors and electronic warfare: an information game for light scout mechs

Scope: sensor and EW mechanics across mech games, naval/sub sims, air combat, and stealth/strategy games, and what transfers to IRONFEUD. Current IRONFEUD baseline: size-based signature (light 0.6 to assault 1.15); firing and jets reveal a mech at full size for 2 s; sensor locks take time and produce per-lance "scan" reports (the AI decides only from scans); light designator marks speed up locks; ECM bubbles block locks and radar sharing; guided missiles need locks; point defense.

Source quality: the best primary-grade sources were the Nebulous: Fleet Command official wiki (Hooded Horse; it documents formulas pulled from the game), the Nuclear Option and VTOL VR wikis (wiki.gg), and MWO's official news posts and patch notes. Most material on MW2/MW4, HBS BattleTech, Cold Waters, DCS and Ace Combat came from fan wikis and forums, and is flagged as such. The session's web-search budget ran out near the end, which left gaps (listed per section).

## 1. Mech games: MW2/MW3/MW4 radar modes, MWO information warfare (and its 2015 rebalance), MW5, Armored Core VI scan, HBS BattleTech sensor lock and stealth

### Takeaway
MWO is the cautionary tale. Its ECM began as a near-hard counter that blanked locks, sharing and the minimap in a 180 m bubble, and it became a must-pick. PGI spent years adding counters (BAP, TAG, NARC, PPC hits, counter-mode ECM), then halved the radius to 90 m, and its own lead designer called the result "shallow". The older MechWarriors' active/passive radar toggle is remembered fondly but poorly documented. What does survive in sources is a clean emit-to-see versus listen-to-hide trade. Armored Core VI and HBS BattleTech show the readable, low-friction versions: a cooldown "scan" pulse, and a sensor lock that spends a turn to strip evasion.

### Cited Findings

**MWO design intent (2012 dev blog)**
- PGI's founding statement: "At its heart, Information Warfare is about controlling the flow of information on the battlefield." The four stated goals were locating enemies, tracking their status, anticipating their intent, and sharing data between units. — [MWO Dev Blog 2: Information Warfare (2012)](https://mwomercs.com/news/2012/01/75-dev-blog-2-information-warfare)
- Same blog: "Target information is now exclusively Line of Sight/Detection (LOSD)", and "If you lose LOSD, you will lose all knowledge that target's position and current status"; target info "decays rapidly". — [MWO Dev Blog 2](https://mwomercs.com/news/2012/01/75-dev-blog-2-information-warfare)
- Planned detection sources: direct sight, radar within an arc, satellite scans ("Orbital scan of the battlefield, highly efficient however still limited to top-down LOSD"), UAVs, dropped detectors, and night/thermal/magnetometer modes (some not planned for launch). Planned ECM roles were spoofers ("Beacons that send out a false target signatures"), disruptors of locks and comms, and surveillance that intercepts enemy intel. — [MWO Dev Blog 2](https://mwomercs.com/news/2012/01/75-dev-blog-2-information-warfare)
- The Role Warfare blog defines scouting as gathering information as a recon/stealth unit and relaying it to the rest of the force. A preview reported that PGI considered role warfare a "pillar" of the game, with scout spotting as the showcase. — [MWO Dev Blog 3: Role Warfare](https://m.mwomercs.com/news/2012/02/107-dev-blog-3-role-warfare); [GameBanshee preview](https://gamebanshee.com/d4hh)

**MWO baseline sensors (as shipped; fan wiki)**
- Standard sensor range is 800 m. Sensors need line of sight and only detect mechs in the front 120° of view (360° only with the Target Retention module). Detected contacts are shared with the team and drawn as triangles on the map and brackets in view. — [MWO Wiki: Sensors](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Sensors)

**MWO Guardian ECM, original 2012 form (fan wiki; added 4 Dec 2012)**
- Default "disrupt" mode makes a 180 m bubble that hides friendlies. Enemies must close to 1/4 of normal range (200 m instead of 800 m) to see hidden mechs, and missile locks on hidden mechs take twice as long. Artemis gives no bonus against them, and NARC sticks but does nothing until the target leaves the bubble. — [MWO Wiki: ECM](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Countermeasure_(ECM))
- Inside a hostile ECM bubble you cannot share targeting data, cannot get any missile locks, TAG gives no bonus, and "battlegrid and targeting information will flicker". — [MWO Wiki: ECM](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Countermeasure_(ECM))
- Between the 180 m bubble edge and the 200 m detection range there is a 20 m "low signal" band where you can detect, target, lock and share. — [MWO Wiki: ECM](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Countermeasure_(ECM))
- Counters: "counter" mode cancels one enemy ECM per counter ECM. A PPC hit disables ECM for 30 s, and repeat hits reset the timer rather than extending it. BAP negates one enemy ECM within 360 m. TAG from outside the bubble allows targeting. Destroying the section or critting the ECM also works. ECM brought HUD status icons: hidden by friendly ECM (an eye), disrupted (low-signal bars), countered (a crossed-out icon). — [MWO Wiki: ECM](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Countermeasure_(ECM))
- Community verdict in 2014 (forum essay replies): even the proposed softer version "still leaves GECM in the state that it is a *must* for any mech that can bring it." The essay's proposals swapped hard blocks for time penalties: +50% lock time on an ECM-protected mech in line of sight, +100% beyond line of sight (indirect LRM), and +100% info-gathering time. NARC "burns through", seismic detects ECM-protected mechs that are moving, and UAVs see everything in their radius. One reply argued that BAP should not hard-counter ECM but should grant short-range, non-line-of-sight detection. — [MWO forum: "ECM: History, Overview and Revision -- An Essay" (2014)](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/171422-ecm-history-overview-and-revision-an-essay)
- A 2013-era forum thread is titled "ECM should not provide stealth 'Mech ability to block an entire team from sensors", which captures the core complaint. — [MWO forum: Fixing Information Warfare](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/87610-fixing-information-warfare/page__view__findpost__p__1672697)

**MWO 2015 rebalance (official PTS post and patch notes)**
- PTS Phase 2 (Oct 2015) set a 500 m baseline sensor range for all mechs, with Sensor Range quirks raising it per variant. Target post-quirk ranges by class: Light ~900 m, Medium ~800 m, Heavy ~700-750 m, Assault ~500-650 m. — [MWO: Mech Re-Balance PTS Phase 2](https://www.mwomercs.com/news/2015/10/1370-mech-rebalance-pts-phase-2)
- Same PTS: info sharing became distance-based, measured from the target to the first teammate and from that teammate to the rest. Lasers no longer did full damage beyond 60% of optimal range against unlocked targets. Reticle feedback on hits now required a lock. ECM radius went from 180 m to 90 m, and ECM "only delays the amount of time it takes to achieve lock-on by 3 seconds" instead of blocking locks. TAG, NARC, BAP, PPCs and counter-mode ECM negated the delay. Target acquisition was set to 0 s for the test. — [MWO: PTS Phase 2](https://www.mwomercs.com/news/2015/10/1370-mech-rebalance-pts-phase-2)
- Lead designer Paul Inouye said the existing IW system was so shallow and underdeveloped that it was hard to use as a balance point, and he wanted IW elements beyond visual target acquisition (reported in search excerpts of the PTS posts; I did not read his exact quote on the page). — [MWO: PTS Phase 2](https://www.mwomercs.com/news/2015/10/1370-mech-rebalance-pts-phase-2)
- What went live on 1 Dec 2015 (patch 1.4.38): only the ECM radius cut is listed. "ECM protective range will be reduced from 180m to 90m." The sensor-range-by-class scheme and the lock-delay rework do not appear in those notes. — [MWO Patch Notes 1.4.38, 01-DEC-2015](https://mwomercs.com/news/2015/11/1420-patch-notes-1438-01dec2015)
- Before the rebalance, a community breakdown said IW-specialist mechs such as the Raven gathered target info twice as fast, and BAP doubled it again (search excerpt). — [MWO forum: True Information Warfare](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/208498-true-information-warfare/Chris%20Currie)

**MWO skill-tree sensor nodes (community forums, 2017-2023)**
- Radar Deprivation and Target Decay counter each other. Deprivation shortens how long an enemy keeps you locked after losing line of sight, and 100% deprivation nullifies target decay. Players value its audio "chirp", which signals that an enemy has lost sight of you. — [MWO forum: Radar Dep](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/286293-radar-dep); [MWO forum: skill nodes ECM vs radar deprivation](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/284085-skill-nodes-ecm-vs-radar-deprivation)
- Seismic sensor (a movement detector) was described as near-universal ("almost all mechs equip the module seismic sensor"). Typical brawler picks were advanced seismic, target info gathering and radar deprivation. LRM boats took target decay, advanced sensors and radar deprivation. A 2017 thread proposed deleting the sensor tree and keeping only zoom, seismic and radar deprivation. — [MWO forum: Nuke the sensor skill tree](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/251258-nuke-the-sensor-skill-tree); [MWO forum: Advice on modules](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/188890-advice-on-modules)

**MWO Scouting mode**
- Faction Play "Scouting" was a 4v4 mode, one mech each, lights and mediums only (2018 community guide). Players called it fun but repetitive. I did not find its win conditions. — [MWO forum: Helpful links for Faction Play (Aug 2018)](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/268303-helpful-links-for-faction-play-august-2018); [MWO forum: multi-objective matches](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/224968-multi-objective-based-game-matches)

**Older MechWarriors: active/passive radar (weakly sourced)**
- A forum poster describes flipping radar between active and passive as "Exactly how it was in MW4 (and MW2?)". The same post proposes MWO numbers: active mode raises your own detectability by 25% and your detection range by 50%. These are a recollection plus a proposal, not MW4 data. — [MWO forum: active/passive sensors and ECM](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/154726-activepassive-sensors-and-ecm)
- MechWarrior: Living Legends (a fan mod in the same lineage): active radar detects to 1000 m, and passive mode cuts your radar range to 300 m. — [MWLL Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.mechlivinglegends.net/index.php/Electronic_Warfare)
- A Sarna entry notes that MW4 mentions the Beagle Active Probe in a mission. I found no MW4 manual text on its range. — [Sarna: Beagle Active Probe](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Bap)

**MW5: Mercenaries**
- GameRevolution's review calls the radar "terrible" because it prevents lock-on missile use unless the enemy is in line of sight, and says poor targeting and sensors make you miss a lot. — [GameRevolution MW5 review](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)

**Armored Core VI: scan**
- Scan is a short-range, cooldown-limited pulse (D-pad down) that reveals nearby enemies and some locations, reportedly through walls. Previews frame it as a way to preview encounters and avoid an obvious first-run ambush. The HUD shows scan recharge, and scanning also reveals collectible parts. These details come from 2023 previews, and I found no official range or cooldown numbers. — [GamePro (DE) tips](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/armored-core-6-tipps-und-tricks,3399614.html); [Wccftech demo deep dive](https://wccftech.com/armored-core-vi-fires-of-rubicon-demo-deep-dive/amp/); [Multiplayer.it interview](https://multiplayer.it/articoli/armored-core-vi-fires-of-rubicon-la-nostra-seconda-intervista-con-il-director-e-il-producer.html)

**HBS BattleTech: sensor lock and ECM (community sources)**
- Sensor Lock uses the spotter's attack action and removes evasion pips. A 2018 Reddit skills guide quotes a developer: "-2 Evasion and a -2 Accuracy". Using it outside combat starts the combat phase. — [r/Battletechgame skills guide](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/Battletechgame/comments/a34bul/skills_guide_updated/eb5wpmw/?context=3); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/637090/discussions/0/1755771809254006257)
- A pilot with the Sensor Lock skill can reveal a sensor-detected enemy to the whole lance without visual contact. Vehicles can be locked as unidentified radar blips without line of sight. — [GameSkinny tips](https://www.gameskinny.com/61a03/battletech-tips-and-strategies); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/637090/discussions/0/1696048245844687849)
- ECM-equipped mechs (Raven, ECM Cataphract) give area effects that need no pilot skill. Players report the AI tries to enter the ECM bubble to negate a stealth charge when it cannot sensor lock. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/637090/discussions/0/2284960483085897674)
- Urban Warfare's Raven 1X merges ECM and active probe in one component. ECM-equipped mechs and nearby allies are "obscured from enemy targeting systems". — [Xbox Store: BATTLETECH Urban Warfare](https://www.xbox.com/en-us/games/store/_/9NTS6BZ3FQ3C)
- A modder notes that HBS set sensor range 33% larger than visual range, and that AI detection misbehaved when modders shrank sensors well below visual range. — [Nexus Mods BattleTech mod 190 posts](https://www.nexusmods.com/battletech/mods/190?tab=posts)

### Inferences
- **MWO's lesson is structural, not numerical.** Blocking locks, sharing and the minimap in one bubble made ECM a binary "on/off" counter to a whole weapon family (LRM/Streak), so it was mandatory. Every later fix replaced *blocks* with *delays* (3 s lock delay, ×2 lock time) and added cheap counters. IRONFEUD's current ECM (blocks locks and radar sharing) sits on MWO's original, failed side. Converting it to "delays and degrades" (longer lock time, coarser and staler scans, a shorter share radius) keeps it valuable without making it a hard counter.
- **MWO's 2015 class sensor ladder (light 900 → assault 500-650 m) is close to IRONFEUD's signature ladder, mirrored.** Lights see farther *and* are seen later. IRONFEUD already scales detectability by size. Adding per-class *sensor range* (lights see farther) would give lights a second, independent edge that bigger mechs can't buy with tonnage.
- **Radar Deprivation's "chirp" is a readability gem.** An audio cue for "the enemy just lost track of you" makes breaking contact a felt, learnable skill. IRONFEUD's 2 s fire/jet reveal could get a matching "you're lit" / "you're dark again" cue.
- **Seismic sensing (detect *movement*, not presence) is the natural counter to ECM.** It also creates a stand-still-versus-move decision for the hidden mech, which fits a size- and speed-based signature model.
- **HBS's sensor lock is a turn-based "spend your attack to strip evasion".** In real time the analogue is a light that stops shooting to paint: the designator mark. Its cost should be visible (the light isn't firing) and its payoff legible (the target's evasion or lock-time debuff shows on the HUD).
- **The AC6 scan pulse is the approachable entry point.** A light's active ping could reveal through walls for a moment but light the pinger up as well (see section 3 on emission).

### Gaps
- No primary MW2/MW3/MW4 manual text was found. Exact active/passive ranges, IFF behaviour, and whether passive mode required shutdown are unverified. The claim that passive mode halves range but keeps you dark unless targeted came from a forum post I could not isolate.
- Live (post-2016) MWO values for ECM, BAP, UAV, NARC/TAG durations, target decay and seismic range were not retrieved. Fandom returned 402 to direct fetch, and the official Dec 2015 notes list only the radius change. Whether the "3 s lock delay" ECM model went live is unconfirmed here.
- MW5 sensor internals (ranges, whether radar is truly LOS-only) were not found beyond the review complaint.
- No official HBS numbers for sensor/visual ranges, ECM radius, or stealth pips.

## 2. Naval and submarine sims: Cold Waters, Dangerous Waters, Silent Hunter (passive vs active sonar, noise vs speed, target motion analysis, readable detection)

### Takeaway
Submarine sims make detection a game through three levers: your own noise rises with speed, passive listening gives bearing only (range has to be *earned* through time and maneuver, which is target motion analysis), and active pinging gives you range at the price of announcing yourself. Environment (thermal layers) adds terrain-like hiding places. For a mech game, the transferable pieces are "speed = noise", "passive = bearing only, refined over time", and "ping = instant fix plus exposure".

### Cited Findings
- Real-world TMA: passive sonar gives only direction. A bearing history over time must be converted into a track by target motion analysis, own-ship maneuvers reduce uncertainty, and a maneuvering target makes TMA harder. — [ATI Courses: Target Motion Analysis](https://aticourses.com/?p=12281)
- Submarine acoustics: below about 8 kts machinery noise dominates, and above about 8 kts flow and propulsion noise take over. — [GlobalSecurity: submarine silencing](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/ship/silent.htm)
- Cold Waters thermal layer (fan site): crossing the layer often breaks sonar contact, and the layer also reduces "*some* transient weapon launch noise". Advice: stay on the opposite side of the layer when fighting Soviet subs, since they hear you less well through it than you hear them. — [PMC Tactical: Cold Waters Thermocline](https://cold-waters.pmctactical.org/thermocline.php)
- Cold Waters players: the layer creates a shadow zone that hides you from surface ASW. Noise on one side is hard to hear from the other, except when directly above or below. The game does not model towed arrays straddling the layer, so you have to move back and forth. — [Steam: Cold Waters thermal layer thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1353742967799693461)
- Cold Waters players (unverified community observations): Silent Running stops noisy crew actions such as reloading and sets about 5 kts, and some say 5 kts is still loud. Players report that noise rises linearly with speed by the same amount for every sub, with no modeled step. Slowing to 10 kts or less lets you hear launch transients. Some Soviet boats on slow battery are nearly inaudible, so you sometimes have to go active to find them. — [Steam: Cold Waters discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/3381662461602302037); [Steam: Cold Waters discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1795152172934268586)
- Cold Waters players: the target's speed is read from TMA. Against an Alfa at flank speed (~42 kts, faster than a Mk 48's ~40 kts transit), the advice is to activate the torpedo so it sprints to 55 kts. — [Steam: Cold Waters discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1499000547481754736)

### Inferences
- **"Speed = noise" maps directly onto IRONFEUD's locomotion.** Footfall impulses already exist, so seismic/acoustic signature could scale with gait speed and tonnage (an assault at a run is the loudest thing on the map). Lights get a real choice: sprint (fast, loud) or creep (slow, quiet). That is the sub-sim core loop, and it matches pillar 1 (weight from motion).
- **Bearing-only passive contacts plus refinement over time** is the readable version of TMA. Show a passive contact as a bearing wedge that narrows the longer, and the more angles, it is observed from. Two lancemates on different bearings collapse it to a point (the cross-fix; see Nebulous ELINT below). This creates a scouting job ("get a second bearing") without any new weapon.
- **Thermal layers ↔ terrain.** Ridges and valleys could act as acoustic or seismic shadows, so terrain use (pillar 3) becomes part of stealth.

### Gaps
- No Cold Waters manual or developer source was found for its noise model, detection formula, or classification UI. Dangerous Waters and Silent Hunter were not researched, because the search budget ran out. Their sonar UIs (waterfall displays, TMA stations) are an expected but unverified reference.

## 3. Air combat sims and arcade: DCS World, Nuclear Option, Ace Combat, Project Wingman, VTOL VR (emission control, RWR, jamming, HUD communication)

### Takeaway
Air games make the emit/listen trade explicit and readable. Your radar reveals you to anyone with a receiver, often at about twice its own range. An RWR gives you bearings and a graded threat state (searched → tracked/locked → missile guiding), with distinct tones. Jamming hides your exact position but advertises that you exist and roughly where, and it draws home-on-jam missiles. The best HUD idioms: colour-graded RWR lines (Nuclear Option's gray/yellow/red) and a dotted "?" box at last known position.

### Cited Findings

**Nuclear Option (indie, wiki.gg)**
- Detection types are optical (passive), active radar (longer range), and passive radar triangulation of your emissions, which has the same range as active radar. Only the EW-25 Medusa can passively triangulate aircraft. — [Nuclear Option Wiki: How detection works](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)
- Team datalink: if any enemy detects you, "all enemies are made aware of your location". A contact that leaves the datalink shows as a dotted box with a question mark at its last known position. Static targets stay on the datalink after detection. — [Nuclear Option Wiki: How detection works](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)
- Optical detection = target visibleRange × observer magnification, gated by a max visualRange (10 km for most aircraft). Example: a 2 km-signature Chicane seen by a 0.7-magnification Revoker is detected at 1.4 km. — [Nuclear Option Wiki: How detection works](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)
- Radar is line-of-sight only. Echo strength falls with distance⁴, so twice the detection range needs 16× the RCS. External weapons raise RCS. Flying within a wingspan of the ground blends into clutter. The radar horizon makes a shadow zone (a radar at 25 m sees to about 19 km). — [Nuclear Option Wiki: How detection works](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)
- RWR: a new radar source gives a beep, then periodic short beeps, orange minimap lines toward the source, and a flashing HUD icon. Bearing is rough, and players triangulate by flying the line. Since 0.31 the lines are colour-coded: gray (searching, not detected), yellow (detected), red (tracked, usually SARH guidance). You can turn your radar off to reduce your signature. — [Nuclear Option Wiki: How detection works](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)
- Jamming pod: jamming *reveals* your position, marked with an X labelled "JAMMING". The Scimitar's home-on-jam lets a jammed radar missile home on the jammer's emissions. — [Nuclear Option Wiki: Radar Jamming Pod](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/Radar_Jamming_Pod)
- Players note that an RWR ping does not mean you are spotted, because the RWR hears emissions from about twice the source's detection range (anecdotal). — [Steam: Nuclear Option discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2168680/discussions/0/603027101813051981)

**VTOL VR (wiki.gg)**
- RWR symbols: primary threat, newest, airborne, locked ("tracking you"; a lock is not a launch), SARH guidance, and ARH guidance (the missile's own seeker has you). Separate tones play for a new source, a search ping (faster when tracked), a hard lock, a SARH lock, and an ARH lock. — [VTOL VR Wiki: RWR](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/RWR)
- A separate infrared Missile Warning System detects launches to about 10 miles, ignores launches heading away, and shows direction arcs. It can false-alarm on missiles aimed at nearby targets, and most aircraft have an overhead blind spot. — [VTOL VR Wiki: RWR](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/RWR)
- Radar detection range scales with RCS × transmitter × receiver. Aspect matters (top and bottom views have a much higher RCS than nose-on), and external stores raise RCS. — [VTOL VR Wiki: Radar (Mechanic)](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/Radar_(Mechanic))
- Jammer modes: NOISE (hides real returns in a cone, and supports allies through corridor jamming), DRFM (false contact clusters that include your true return; the only mode effective against single-target locks), and SAS (spoofs a different aircraft identity, with power tuned so the apparent range fits). Burn-through range = radar power × RCS / jammer power. Corridor jamming falls off with angle: 76% at 3°, 47% at 5°, 4.7% at 10°. Noise blocks your own fire-control radar. Some missiles switch to home-on-jam, and the counter is to turn the jammer off when a missile is inbound. — [VTOL VR Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/Jamming)

**DCS World (community forums; treat as player reports)**
- DCS RWRs give three alert levels, the lowest being a search warning for periodic illumination. Players note a lock is not a launch, and that an AMRAAM fired from track-while-scan shows only a search warning until the missile goes active. That produces "sudden" launch warnings, which are realistic but feel unfair. — [DCS forum: RWR launch warning](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/349725-rwr-launch-warning/); [DCS forum: how to know which plane is locking you](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/217632-how-to-know-which-plane-is-locking-you-up)

**Ace Combat / Project Wingman (arcade)**
- Ace Combat jamming is mostly *mission terrain*: stationary jammer stations disable lock-on near them (AC Zero's Tauberg, AC04's Stonehenge), and AC X's Nevera Jammer causes radar and comms loss over a large radius. In AC7, enemy Growlers hinder the player's weapons, but "they were not completely disabled". — [Ace Combat Wiki: Radar jamming](https://acecombat.wiki.gg/wiki/Radar_jamming)
- The series' ECM special weapon briefly lowers enemy targeting in range and then needs a recharge. Allied ESM aircraft in Infinity/AC7 extend datalink, lock speed and lock range, but enemy ECM negates ESM in AC7. — [Ace Combat Wiki: ECM](https://acecombat.wiki.gg/wiki/ECM); [Ace Combat Wiki: Electronic warfare](https://acecombat.wiki.gg/wiki/Electronic_warfare)
- Project Wingman: I found nothing on its radar or ECM model, only user feedback that missile-lock and incoming-missile cues are mainly audio. — [Project Wingman itch.io comments](https://rb-d2.itch.io/wingman/comments?before=414)

### Inferences
- **The emitter/receiver range asymmetry is the core of emit vs listen.** If a passive receiver hears your active sensor at 1.25-2× the range at which your sensor sees *it* (Nuclear Option, Highfleet, Nebulous all do this), then going active is a deliberate choice: you see now, but you are heard first. A light in passive mode, listening for an enemy's active radar, gets bearing warnings before the enemy's sensors can reach it. That is a skill-based scouting advantage lights can own.
- **Graded, colour-coded threat states** (gray searched → yellow detected → red locked/missile) are the right HUD grammar for IRONFEUD. They also make the AI's knowledge readable: a yellow line means "the enemy lance has a scan on you"; red means "a lock is being built for missiles".
- **The dotted "?" last-known-position box** is the cheapest possible fix for "why did that contact vanish?", and it fits IRONFEUD's scan reports directly (a scan ages into a ghost box with a timestamp).
- **Jamming must announce the jammer.** Nuclear Option's "X JAMMING" marker, VTOL VR's home-on-jam, and Nebulous's jam lines of bearing all keep EW from being free stealth. An IRONFEUD ECM bubble should hide *details* (exact position, scan contents) but show a bearing or area to the jammer. Optionally, a guided weapon could home on it.
- **Ace Combat's arcade lesson:** jamming works best as a *map objective* (jammer towers that suppress locks in an area until a light kills or disables them), which gives lights a mission job the assaults can't do quickly.
- **Avoid DCS's "silent launch" surprise** in an arcade-sim mech game. A missile lock on you should always give a warning, which is consistent with IRONFEUD's 1 s arc-missile lock track.

### Gaps
- No primary DCS documentation (ED manuals) was read. Project Wingman's EW is unknown. No Nuclear Option devlog was found; the wiki is the source.

## 4. Stealth and strategy games: Shadow Tactics, Sins/Homeworld, Battlefleet Gothic: Armada, Highfleet, Carrier Command 2, Nebulous: Fleet Command, Children of a Dead Earth

### Takeaway
Nebulous: Fleet Command and Highfleet are the two most relevant references. Nebulous turns sensors into deterministic, inspectable physics: radar return ∝ 1/d⁴, jamming that changes the law to 1/d², jammers that leave a line of bearing, burn-through pulses, cross-fixed passive bearings, staged intel "packets" (≈ IRONFEUD scans), and track quality. Highfleet turns the same ideas into a minimal, readable strategic game: radar sweeps reveal you to enemy ELINT, ELINT gives bearing and strength only, and jamming is not stealth. Shadow Tactics shows how to make perception *inspectable*: click an enemy to see its cone.

### Cited Findings

**Nebulous: Fleet Command (official Hooded Horse wiki)**
- Radar return power Pr = Pt·Gt²·σ·A / (16π²·d⁴). Detection must beat ambient noise, signal-to-noise against jamming, and sensitivity. Sensors sweep for new contacts about once per second, unlocked tracks update every 0.25 s, and locked tracks every physics frame. — [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)
- Under jamming, detection range scales with the *square root* of RCS (the 1/2 exponent) instead of the fourth root, "a jammed radar experiences significantly different scaling laws". Stand-off jamming from behind the target has a different (1/4-power) law. — [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar); [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare)
- Jammer stacking has diminishing returns: each additional jammer is multiplied by e^-(n/2.75)², so the total never exceeds about 2.94× the strongest jammer (2nd adds 0.876, 3rd 0.589, 4th 0.304...). — [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare)
- A jammer creates a "jammed volume" on the victim's sensor: a box 2·Dj long toward the spotter and 2·Dj·Er wide (Er = 0.4 for vanilla jammers). Only tracks inside that box are jammed, so "offset spotters" moving laterally out of the cone can still see and relay. "A team spread across a wide area is much more resistant to radar jamming compared to a team that is tightly grouped." — [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare)
- Jamming is not invisibility: most search radars produce a jamming line of bearing toward the jammer, which remembers the last known range if a track was held within 30 s of jamming starting. Two bearings can be cross-fixed by a strobe correlator into a lockable track. — [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare)
- Counters to jamming: close the range, use fire-control radars, use burn-through sweeps (a single high-power pulse that makes a 30 s snapshot "burn track", where the target knows it was hit by a burn sweep), use illuminators ("one Floodlight defeats one jammer"), or use passive and visual sensors (about 3 km visual around every ship). Jamming only affects active radar. — [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare); [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)
- ELINT passively detects enemy radars at up to 1.25× that radar's max range. One ship gets a rough bearing, two ships cross-fix, and ELINT also identifies the emitter category (search/track, fire control, early warning, long-range tracking). Sidelobes allow detection at reduced range even when the radar isn't pointed at you. ELINT cannot detect jamming or illuminators. — [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)
- Comms jamming saturates the receiver, so a jammed ship can still *transmit* but not *receive*. Comms jammers are occluded by terrain, and a relay ship outside the cone keeps jammed ships indirectly connected. — [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare)
- Decoys: chaff creates a stationary false contact (~22,000 m² signature) that decays to ~14,500 m² at 38 s and expires at 45 s. An active decoy moves at 20 m/s for 30 s with ~400,000 m² signature. Decoy containers that mimic ships "cannot be revealed by intel alone, and will require visual spotting." — [Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare); [Nebulous Wiki: Intelligence](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Intelligence)
- Intel is staged. A new track is "Unknown". Then come Warship, Class, Hull and Name, each costing 200 effort, plus a parallel 200-effort status report (condition %, point-defense %, debuffs) that must be re-gathered whenever a reported field changes. Effort rates run from 1 pt/s (basic) to 15-19.5 pt/s (intel centre). Only the best source counts, intel is shared team-wide, and status figures carry a ± inaccuracy (e.g., true COND 66% shown as 46-86% at 20% inaccuracy). — [Nebulous Wiki: Intelligence](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Intelligence)
- Track memory: if a lost track is re-acquired within 2.5 km of its last known position, all intel is kept. Otherwise it gets a new track number and starts over. — [Nebulous Wiki: Intelligence](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Intelligence)
- Track quality is graded TQ15 (≤4 m error) to TQ1 (>152 m). Search-radar error shrinks with signal-to-noise, velocity error scales with target speed, and multiple radars on the network keep the best guess. — [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)
- RCS depends on aspect: it is greatest from above, the sides or behind, and lowest about 45° off the bow. Firing big guns "blooms" signature (e.g., +1500%). — [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)
- A beginner guide notes the blanket jammer works in a directional cone, ships outside it stay visible, and home-on-jam missiles are beaten by turning your own jamming off. — [KosGames: Nebulous beginners' guide](https://kosgames.com/?p=385)

**Highfleet (fan guides)**
- Radar: a single sweep (the "1" button) or continuous (the infinity button); sector mode scans a 60° cone faster. There is an overall radar range plus a smaller *tracking* range. Tracked enemies show on the map with exact heading and velocity, and only tracked targets get accurate SARH missile guidance. — [GamePretty: Highfleet radar guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radar-aircraft-ship-systems-and-nuclear-warfare/); [GamePretty: Highfleet radios/EWAR guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radios-warfare-and-ewar/)

"THE ELINT PICKING UP A SIGNAL DOES NOT MEAN THAT YOU ARE DETECTED." — [GamePretty: Highfleet radar guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radar-aircraft-ship-systems-and-nuclear-warfare/); [GamePretty: EWAR guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radios-warfare-and-ewar/)
- Tactics: pulse the radar once and then change course. Detached strike groups with their own radars draw detection away from the main fleet. — [GamePretty: EWAR guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radios-warfare-and-ewar/)

- The map view draws a yellow ring for enemy tracking range and has a toggle that shows sudden-strike probability. ### Inferences
- **Nebulous's intel packets are a near-exact template for IRONFEUD's scans.** Stage them: (1) contact/bearing, (2) weight class, (3) model, (4) loadout, plus a status report (section damage %, heat state) that goes stale and must be refreshed when it changes. Add a ± inaccuracy that shrinks with a better sensor or a designator mark. Lights, BAP-like gear and marks raise the effort rate. This turns "a light is near the enemy" into graded, visible value: the lance sees the enemy assault's damaged left torso *because* the light held a scan.
- **The "re-acquire within radius keeps intel" rule** (2.5 km in Nebulous; scaled to mech maps, roughly 50-150 m) means breaking contact briefly is cheap, but relocating far makes you "new" again. That rewards lights that move a long way, not merely a few steps behind cover.
- **Jamming as a cone or volume from the jammer toward each victim**, not a sphere, creates *geometry*. Flanking or offset spotters defeat it, which fits pillar 3 (teamwork and terrain) and gives a light a counter-EW job: get off-axis and relay. IRONFEUD's sphere-shaped ECM bubble could keep the sphere for self-protection but cast degrading "shadows" rather than hard blocks.
- **Burn-through as a costed action:** a one-off high-power ping that pierces ECM, yields a snapshot (not a live track), and alerts the target that it was hit. That is an excellent active-scout ability and a natural counter to ECM.
- **Diminishing jammer stacking** (≤ ~3× the strongest) prevents "ECM lance" degeneracy, a direct guard against the "lance of X" anti-goal.
- **Decoys that a scan can't unmask but a visual can** give lights a reason to get eyes on.
- **Highfleet's "a strength bar is not a range"** is a great readable ambiguity. A passive warning tells you *someone is emitting toward you*, not how close they are. Its explicit caption ("picking up a signal does not mean you are detected") is a good tutorial line to borrow in spirit.
- **Shadow Tactics' click-to-inspect cone** suggests a debug-and-player tool: hover over an enemy contact to see its estimated sensor envelope, given your signature and their mode. IRONFEUD already treats debug visualisation as first-class, so that overlay can double as player-facing information in an easy mode.

## 5. Design lessons: emit vs listen, signature from speed/heat/firing, burn-through, decoys, false contacts, information decay, HUD readability, keeping EW from being mandatory or a hard counter

### Takeaway
Across genres, the information game works when (a) seeing has a price (emissions are heard farther than they see), (b) hiding has a price (slow, cold, not firing, not emitting), (c) every concealment tool advertises *something* (bearing, area, "a jammer is here"), (d) information is graded and decays visibly rather than vanishing, and (e) counters are many, soft and positional, not a single binary item. MWO's ECM history is the counter-example on every point.

### Cited Findings
- **Emit vs listen asymmetry:** ELINT and passive receivers hear an emitter at 1.25× its range (Nebulous), about 2× (Highfleet), or at the emitter's full active range via triangulation (Nuclear Option). — [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar); [GamePretty: Highfleet radar](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radar-aircraft-ship-systems-and-nuclear-warfare/); [Nuclear Option Wiki](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)
- **Signature grows with activity:** external weapons raise RCS (VTOL VR, Nuclear Option). Firing railguns or mass drivers blooms signature (Nebulous). Your own noise rises with speed (Cold Waters, community). Radar-off reduces signature (Nebulous, Nuclear Option). — [VTOL VR Wiki: Radar](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/Radar_(Mechanic)); [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar); [Steam: Cold Waters](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/3381662461602302037)
- **Burn-through as a formula and as an action:** VTOL VR's burn-through range = radar power × RCS / jammer power. Nebulous's burn sweep is a one-off power multiplier that yields a 30 s snapshot. — [VTOL VR Wiki: EW](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/Jamming); [Nebulous Wiki: Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)
- **Jamming announces itself:** Nuclear Option's "X JAMMING" marker, Highfleet's "jamming does not provide stealth", Nebulous's jam lines of bearing, and home-on-jam in all three air and space games. — [Nuclear Option Wiki: Jamming Pod](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/Radar_Jamming_Pod); [GamePretty: EWAR](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radios-warfare-and-ewar/); [Nebulous Wiki: EW](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare); [VTOL VR Wiki: EW](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/Jamming)
- **False contacts:** VTOL VR's DRFM makes clusters of false contacts that include the true return. Nebulous chaff decays over 45 s, and its ship-mimic decoys need visual confirmation. MWO's 2012 plan included "spoofers" sending false signatures (never shipped as such). — [VTOL VR Wiki: EW](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/Jamming); [Nebulous Wiki: EW](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare); [MWO Dev Blog 2](https://mwomercs.com/news/2012/01/75-dev-blog-2-information-warfare)
- **Information decay made visible:** Nuclear Option's dotted "?" box at last known position, Nebulous's 30 s burn-track snapshots and status reports that go stale, and MWO's "decays rapidly" plus target decay / radar deprivation skills. — [Nuclear Option Wiki](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works); [Nebulous Wiki: Intelligence](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Intelligence); [MWO forum: Radar Dep](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/286293-radar-dep)
- **HUD readability grammar:** colour-coded RWR states (gray/yellow/red), distinct tones per threat state (VTOL VR), MWO's ECM status icons (hidden eye, disrupted bars, countered crossed icon), and Shadow Tactics' click-to-see cones. — [Nuclear Option Wiki](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works); [VTOL VR Wiki: RWR](https://vtolvr.wiki.gg/wiki/RWR); [MWO Wiki: ECM](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Countermeasure_(ECM)); [TechRaptor: Shadow Tactics](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/shadow-tactics-blades-of-shogun-review-brilliance-in-motion)
- **Mandatory or hard counter (negative evidence):** MWO's lock-blocking 180 m ECM became a "must" pick, and PGI moved to a 90 m radius and proposed lock *delays* instead of blocks. Homeworld's cloak counters were near-mandatory in multiplayer. — [MWO forum essay](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/171422-ecm-history-overview-and-revision-an-essay); [MWO PTS Phase 2](https://www.mwomercs.com/news/2015/10/1370-mech-rebalance-pts-phase-2); [Ludo.guide DoK](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/homeworld-deserts-of-kharak/homeworld-deserts-of-kharak-part-10)
- **LOS-only radar frustration:** the MW5 review's complaint, and MWO forum calls for "a return to a more omni-directional, non-LOS based radar system as seen in prior Mechwarrior titles" (search excerpt). — [GameRevolution MW5](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review); [MWO forum: Vision, Radar and Target locks](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/277734-vision-radar-and-target-locks)

### Inferences (concrete proposals for IRONFEUD, in priority order)
1. **Two sensor postures per mech, not one global LOS rule.**
   - *Passive* (default): visual and short-range line of sight, plus "hearing" active emitters as bearing wedges at ~1.5-2× their range. You are detected only by your physical signature (size × speed/footfall noise × firing/jet reveal).
   - *Active*: radar range ×1.5, and non-LOS detection within a short radius (the BAP-like non-LOS idea from the MWO thread). You emit, though, so every enemy receiver gets your bearing well beyond your own radar range.

   This revives the beloved MW2/MW4 toggle with a reason to use both. The AI uses the same toggle through `MechInput`.
2. **Signature = size × motion × activity, on one meter.** IRONFEUD already has size and a 2 s reveal for firing and jets. Add continuous terms for gait speed (footfall impulse energy, so heavies running are loud), heat (a hot mech is visible on thermal), and emission (active mode). Show it on the player's HUD as one "how loud am I" bar with ticks for each contributor. Readable and learnable.
3. **Graded contacts instead of seen/unseen.** Use the states bearing-only → located → classified (weight class) → identified (model/loadout) → status (section damage, heat), each costing scan time (Nebulous packets). Show each state with a distinct HUD glyph. Contacts age into last-known ghosts (a dotted box with seconds since the last scan) and don't vanish. Re-acquiring near the ghost keeps the scan level. The AI already decides only from scans, so this makes its knowledge displayable in the debug overlay too (pillar: debug-drawable decisions).
4. **ECM: from block to degrade, and make it findable.**
   - Inside or behind an ECM field, enemies get +X% lock time, coarser and staler scans, and a slower or shorter share radius (MWO's 2015 direction).
   - A jammer shows as a bearing line or area marker ("someone is jamming from there").
   - Stacking has diminishing returns (≤ ~2-3× the strongest).
   - The field casts a cone or shadow from the jammer toward each enemy sensor, so an off-axis spotter sees through it (Nebulous jammed volume).

   Counters are many and soft: a designator mark, a burn-through ping, closing range, a seismic/footfall listener, a hit on the ECM component (a crit effect, which IRONFEUD already has).
5. **Light-exclusive verbs.**
   - Burn-through ping: a one-off pulse that pierces ECM, gives a 20-30 s snapshot, and alerts the pinged mechs.
   - Cross-fix: two lancemates' passive bearings resolve to a position, which teaches spreading out (pillar 3 teamwork).
   - Decoy/spoofer: a false contact that scans can't unmask but eyes can, decaying over ~30-45 s.
   - Optional seismic listen: stand still to hear moving mechs through terrain, which means "stop to listen".

   Each verb gives lights a job assaults can't do (pillar 4) without making lights *required*. A lance without a scout still fights on visual and short scans, just with less information.
6. **HUD threat grammar.** Gray/yellow/red bearing ticks: gray = an enemy emitter heard; yellow = you are in an enemy scan; red = a lock is building, or a missile is guiding on you. Each gets a distinct tone, plus a "dark again" chirp when the last enemy scan on you lapses (MWO radar deprivation). One line of HUD text answers "why did it vanish?": "LOST - last seen 6 s".
7. **Anti-mandatory guardrails to verify headlessly.**
   - Run the balance matrix with and without ECM lights and scouts. A no-scout lance should lose information-heavy missions more often but still win brawl/defend missions.
   - No EW item should change win rate more than a weapon swap does.
   - Mission types that reward information (recon, escort-through-fog, raid) should be part of the mission × composition matrix CLAUDE.md requires.

- There is no quantitative evidence (win rates, telemetry) on how much EW should matter. MWO's history is qualitative, from forum sentiment plus PGI's own actions.
