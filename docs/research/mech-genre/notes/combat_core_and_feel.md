# Combat core and "feel" of the mech-sim genre (MechWarrior, HBS BattleTech, Armored Core VI, Titanfall, Hawken and others)

Scope note: this covers the mechanics that define the genre's feel, how the main games implemented them, and what players and critics loved or hated. Franchise mech and weapon names appear only as citations. Every finding is framed as a transferable principle for IRONFEUD's original content. Sourcing quality was uneven. Several primary pages blocked automated fetches (MWO official wiki redirects to a landing page, Prima Games 403, nav-alpha empty). Many numbers therefore rest on fan wikis and forums; where that is the case it is flagged. Items I recall but could not verify are in the Gaps sections, never in the Cited Findings.

## 1. Which mechanics do players and critics name as essential to the mech-sim feel, and how did MW2, MW4, MWO and MW5 differ on them?

### Takeaway
Critics keep naming the same core of the "sim heart": throttle plus independent torso facing; showing your strong armour; heat as a constant budget; damage by section, with weapons lost to their limbs; weapon mount height mattering against cover; and physical scale (smashing trees, cars and buildings). The most-cited gaps in the modern games are physical contact: melee, collision and knockdowns. Those are also the areas the series has repeatedly removed or simplified.

### Cited Findings
**Throttle, torso twist, facing, mount height (the core loop critics praise)**
- MW5: Mercenaries review: "throttle and orientation control are a key part of the simulator-style play". Twisting the torso "to strafe targets and show strong armor to the enemy is key". Weapon layout matters: high lasers may "shoot over a wall" while low arm weapons "explode the cover you were using." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercenaries review (Bolding, 80/100)](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)
- MW5: Clans review: the mech's "facing, throttle, and controls once again the centerpiece of combat". Even on simple FPS-like controls, "its height and the physical placement of its guns really matter": a head-height cockpit can see over a building while chest weapons hit the upper storeys. Fun comes from "caring about armor, facings, and managing your mech's heat output so you don't shut down." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans review (82/100)](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)
- MW2 (1995): legs and upper body move independently, so you walk one way and aim another. The retrospective calls this a skill that takes practice. Mechs "turn slowly and carry momentum," and players can over-rotate the torso or lose their bearings. — [Generation Amiga, MW2 retrospective (Jul 2026)](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/07/25/mechwarrior-2-retrospective-review-why-it-became-a-pc-gaming-milestone/)
- Wikipedia describes MW2 as a tactical simulation that includes the physical simulation of the player's mech. The console ports tweaked the mechanics "to emphasize action over the tactical simulation". — [Wikipedia, MechWarrior 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MechWarrior_2:_31st_Century_Combat)

**Weight and acceleration feel**
- MW5 Clans: "a real sense of weight to how machines accelerate and decelerate". "Each new mech you pilot has its own quirks." The reviewer calls it "the best that handling a BattleMech has ever felt", but footstep and explosion sound design is "anemic" and undercuts it. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans review](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)
- MW2's heaviness comes from deliberate movement, limited heat capacity and component damage, so "Every shot has a cost. Every mistake leaves a mark." — [Generation Amiga](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/07/25/mechwarrior-2-retrospective-review-why-it-became-a-pc-gaming-milestone/)

**Environment destruction and scale**
- MW5 Clans has "the best physics of a MechWarrior game to date". A 25 t light tramples cars and smashes treelines. A 100 t assault "plows through entire buildings a smaller mech uses as cover", and falling concrete chunks the size of a small mech are no obstacle for a big one. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)
- MW5 Mercs: "Fights ... feel intensely physical as your mech slows to scale inclines or ploughs through a row of parked cars". Lasers leave "a trail of falling, burning trees". A dodging mech "shoulder checks a building and obliterates the facade." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercs](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)

**Melee, collision, knockdowns (the recurring hole)**
- MW5 Mercs at launch had no melee. Mechs "collide with a crash of screeching metal and… awkwardly grind against each other". A claw-armed starter mech's arm "just hangs limply". The reviewer did find running over small tanks and jump-jet Death From Above "suitably satisfying." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercs](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)
- The MW5 Call to Arms DLC (June 2022) added 9 melee weapons (axe, swords, hatchet, claw, mace and others) plus melee-focused variants. It builds on melee first added in the Legend of the Kestrel Lancers DLC. — [Sarna news roundup June 2022](https://www.sarna.net/news/your-battletech-news-roundup-for-june-2022/); [GOGDB release notes](https://www.gogdb.org/product/2147483045/releasenotes). Players call melee very strong. One noted that the best use of hatchets and swords is "taking arms off." — [r/Mechwarrior5 thread (Mar 2025)](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/Mechwarrior5/comments/1j3b9y1/are_melee_weapons_op) (forum, anecdotal)
- MWO removed collision and knockdowns in 2013. PGI's official reason was "back to basics", rebuilding the functionality correctly and simplifying movement code. Players also cite abuse. Others fear that ramming knockdowns would let heavies "bowl over" lights. After the removal, players complained that lights could ram through or clip past mechs freely. — [MWO forum, re-implement knockdown](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/156246-re-implement-knockdown/page__view__findpost__p__3312252); [MWO forum 2016, knockdowns and alpha strikes](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/236225-mech-knock-downs-and-alpha-strikes/)
- In December 2016, PGI said that making an AI-controlled assault move reliably for the Escort mode "necessitated a re-evaluation of 'Mech collision code as a whole". AI navigation and collision are coupled. — [MWO December 2016 Roadmap](https://online.mwomercs.com/news/2016/12/1670-december-roadmap-beyond)
- Tabletop BattleTech: a lost leg or hip actuator adds piloting-roll penalties, and a destroyed leg forces a roll to avoid falling. — [BattleTech AGOAC reference card](https://battletech.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AGOAC-Reference-Card-7th-printing.pdf)

**Cockpit, HUD, voice**
- MW2's cockpit "fills the screen with gauges, warnings and targeting data" but stays readable: armour, radar contacts, weapon status, heat, speed and target data, each with a direct purpose. Warnings come in a "calm mechanical voice" over the "constant hum of the machine". — [Generation Amiga](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/07/25/mechwarrior-2-retrospective-review-why-it-became-a-pc-gaming-milestone/)
- MW5's drop sequence is singled out as "superb": the mech rotating into position in the bay, the pilot's hands on the boot-up sequence, and a spoken systems checklist as the doors open. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercs](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)
- Fans complain that MW5 lacked HOTAS and pedal support that older entries had. One argues "cockpits are cosmetic in every mechwarrior title." — [Steam/forum aggregation via search; Steam MW5 Clans discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2000890/discussions/0/4625853880055464882) (fan opinion)

### Inferences
- IRONFEUD already covers most of the named "sim heart" (twist, facing armour, heat, sections, signature, scale by class). The clearest gaps against the genre are **physical contact**: melee or physical attacks, mech-to-mech collision consequences, knockdowns and falls, and stomping vehicles. MW5 shows that even crude versions (Death From Above, running over tanks) are memorable. MWO shows that knockdowns are hard to balance in competitive play. IRONFEUD is single-player with procedural legs, so it is unusually well placed to do falls properly: a stability budget feeding the gait solver and springs, not a canned animation.
- **Mount height versus cover** is a praised, emergent mechanic. IRONFEUD has per-section hit spheres and a recessed cockpit, so it should check that shots originate from real mount points and that the AI reasons about "my arm guns clear this wall, my torso guns don't."
- **Scale through destruction**: the most-praised "feel of mass" moments are big mechs ignoring cover that small ones need (trees, walls, buildings). This also supports the weight-class rule: assaults can delete cover, lights can't. That is a terrain-shaping role, not raw stat superiority.
- Footfall and explosion audio is where even the best-handling entry was criticised. IRONFEUD's layered footfall plan is a real lever.

### Gaps
- No official spec found for MW2, MW3 or MW4 fall, knockdown or leg-loss behaviour (I recall MW3 had falls and MW4 "hobbled" mechs on leg loss, but no reliable source turned up).
- No numbers found for MW5 melee damage or collision rules.

## 2. How do sensors, radar, ECM, target-info gathering and lancemate commands work, and what did players think of them?

### Takeaway
Sensors are where the modern games have been weakest. Reviewers attacked MW5's line-of-sight-only radar and the lack of a lead indicator, and fans miss the older active/passive radar toggle. Lancemate command depth was MW5's other big complaint. Clans answered it with three command channels (F-keys, a radial menu and an overhead "Battle Grid" RTS view) but kept weak AI. Critics consistently treat "more control over dumb AI" as better than "smart AI you can't steer."

### Cited Findings
- MW5 Mercs (GameRevolution): enemies are detected only within line of sight and drop off the minimap when you look away. Radar called "horrendous". The crosshair is "almost completely worthless" with no lead indicator, so you aim by feel. Lock-on missiles suffer from the same radar limits. — [GameRevolution, MW5 Mercs review](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)
- Older MechWarrior titles let you "flip your radar between active and passive", which MWO lacks. Fans call this "real sensor modes like Battletech." — [MWO forum, active/passive sensors and ECM](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/154726-activepassive-sensors-and-ecm); [MWO forum, let's talk about sensors](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/220847-lets-talk-about-sensors/) (fan recollection, not manuals)
- MWO active probe and ECM interactions are contested in the community. One claim: in active mode a probe detects (doesn't counter) ECM within 200 m. Another: it disables the nearest ECM. A complaint: the probe is useless inside an ECM's "Low Signal Strength" range. The lore says the light probe adds 200 m detection range. — [MWO forum](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/154726-activepassive-sensors-and-ecm); [Sarna, Beagle Active Probe](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/BAP); [MechLivingLegends wiki, Active Radar Probes](https://wiki.mechlivinglegends.net/index.php?title=BHP). Sources conflict.
- Lore: pilots can turn heat sinks down to cut infrared visibility. Partially submerged mechs can shut down heat sinks above the waterline to hide their thermal signature. — [Sarna, Heat sink](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Heat_sink)
- MW5 Clans "addresses a big frustration from MechWarrior 5" with three ways to give orders: function keys, a middle-mouse radial menu, and the Battle Grid overhead RTS-style view that "seamlessly pops up". AI on both sides is "still fairly weak", but the extra control means "if one weird guy in your squad is standing behind a hill doing nothing you can easily get their mech into range and line of sight." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)
- CGMagazine praised the Clans command systems for "technical depth". Its main flaw: "gameplay doesn't stop while you issue commands." — [CGMagazine, MW5 Clans review](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/mechwarrior-5-clans-pc-review)
- MW5 Mercs players found the early command set "barebones, just the 4 commands". A 2024 Clans player said the only commands they needed were guard location, follow me, and attack my target. A 2021 forum request asked for "defend navpoint" and "force eject". — [Steam MW5 Clans discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2000890/discussions/0/4691153988128083747); [MWO forum, commands for the lance mate](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/281522-commands-for-the-lance-mate)
- MW4 Mercenaries: lancemates are controlled with F-keys and "follow your orders well" (GameWatcher 2002). A pre-release Q&A promised mid-mission tutorials for lancemate commands. A preview says you command two full lances (8 mechs) versus one lance of 4 before. Counterpoint: an EVGA forum review lists "lancemate AI is non existent" as a con. — [GameWatcher MW4 Mercs review](https://www.gamewatcher.com/mechwarrior-4-mercenaries/review); [GameSpot MW4 Mercs Q&A](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/mechwarrior-4-mercenaries-qanda/1100-2874574/); [GameSpot preview](https://gamespot.com/articles/mechwarrior-4-mercenaries-preview/1100-2881968/); [EVGA forum](https://forums.evga.com/Mechwarrior-Mercenaries-Quick-and-Dirty-Review-m3020647.aspx)
- MW5 Clans gives each pilot distinct skills. One is good with ECM and radar, which suits recon or ranged support. You can swap between pilots mid-mission. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)
- Night and weather as sensor terrain: in MW5 Mercs raids, night vision hampered enemies' long-range shots, and dust storms shielded movement. The reviewer built fast mechs to weave through cover around this. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercs](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)

### Inferences
- IRONFEUD's shared lance scans and size-based signature already go beyond MW5. The lesson from MW5's radar criticism is that **the player must be able to read the sensor model**. If contacts vanish, the HUD should show *why* (lost line of sight, ECM, last-known position decaying), or players read it as broken.
- An **active/passive (emit vs listen) toggle** is a cheap, beloved depth knob that fits IRONFEUD's "signature by size, firing reveals you" model. Passive mode lowers your signature but shrinks your detection. Pairs naturally with lights as scouts.
- **Weather and night as sensor modifiers** give lights a situational job (raids, infiltration) without stat buffs. That matches CLAUDE.md goal #4.
- Commands: the minimum viable set fans actually use is attack my target, follow me, hold or guard position. Beyond that, the praised addition is an **overhead tactical map with slowed or paused time** (CGMagazine's complaint). In single-player, a time-slow or pause in the command view is low-risk and directly answers the main complaint.
- A lead indicator: GameRevolution's complaint suggests offering an optional lead pip, possibly gated by sensor lock quality. That rewards IRONFEUD's scan system.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve MW2 or MW4 manuals for the exact sensor-mode rules, radar ranges or lancemate command lists (I recall MW4 had F-key commands such as attack my target, form on me, hold fire, go to nav point, but couldn't verify).
- No primary source for MWO's current sensor and info-gathering rules (target info gathering time, radar deprivation). The official wiki was inaccessible.

## 3. Ammo vs energy vs missile tradeoffs, weapon groups and chain-fire, cooling, critical hits and ammo explosions: what design purpose did each serve?

### Takeaway
Heat is the master constraint that makes energy weapons "free but hot" and ballistics or missiles "cool but finite and explosive." Every game that let players stack identical weapons got boat-and-alpha metas. Each one then added a corrective layer (MWO's ghost heat, Hawken's overheat lockout) that players called opaque or frustrating. Weapon identity through projectile behaviour (beam duration, velocity, drop, missile flight paths) is what critics praise most in the newest entry.

### Cited Findings
- MW2: each weapon adds heat, and careless firing shuts the mech down "to protect itself", which is far worse near enemies. Energy weapons need no ammo but run hotter. Ballistics and missiles hit hard but have limited supply. — [Generation Amiga](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/07/25/mechwarrior-2-retrospective-review-why-it-became-a-pc-gaming-milestone/)
- MWO ghost heat ("heat scaling") exists "to reduce the prevalence of alpha strike builds ... and high DPS builds". It triggers when a group fires more than two PPCs, ER PPCs, large or ER large lasers, more than one AC/20, any three SRMs, or six medium lasers, plus other combinations. "Each additional weapon fired will contribute to the heat output more significantly than the last." — [MWO Fandom wiki, Heat Scaling](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Heat_Scaling) (fan wiki, read via browser)
- Players criticise ghost heat as "not entirely transparent and is seemingly arbitrary". Penalties are per linked weapon group rather than per mech, which is easy to work around by staggering fire. Proposals have suggested replacing it with accuracy or cooldown penalties. — [MechSpecs, Ghost Heat](https://mechspecs.com/posts/82197/); [MWO forum, ghost heat simplified](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/278124-ghost-heat-simplified); [MWO forum, remove ghost heat](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/143302-remove-ghost-heat/page__st__100__p__4125349)
- MWO over 100% heat: automatic shutdown. A large overshoot causes incremental internal damage to the centre torso that bypasses armour. Override prevents shutdown, but the mech takes "severe internal damage every second" while above 100%, randomly assigned to non-cockpit sections. Players report damage grows with the overage, and that a very deep shutdown can kill you. — [MWO forum, understanding heat (quotes official wiki)](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/175656-understanding-heat); [MWO forum, death by overheating](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/120639-death-by-overheating) (exact thresholds anecdotal)
- Map and ambient heat (MWO and Living Legends): ambient temperature sets a floor heat can't drop below and scales dissipation. Water's high conductivity speeds cooling. Cold, low-conductivity maps can cool about 20% slower. Lava heats you. Living Legends' coolant flush is a limited, key-triggered dump refilled on repair, best used at high heat. — [MechLivingLegends wiki, Heat](https://wiki.mechlivinglegends.net/index.php?title=Heat); [MWO forum, it's getting hot in here](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/143356-its-getting-hot-in-here)
- Lore basis: water is a much better coolant than air. Cold-climate and submerged mechs dissipate heat better, and desert mechs may need refits. Double heat sinks dissipate twice as much per ton but take more space. — [Sarna, Heat sink](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Heat_sink)
- MW5 Mercs at launch (GameRevolution): heat built so fast that mechs often shut down after a few shots. Laser-armed mechs were "almost useless", and heat sinks ate the weight needed for weapons or armour. — [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review). (Later patches and mods changed this; I did not verify the specifics.)
- MW5 AI pilots hold fire when a shot would overheat them, so AI loadouts need careful heat budgeting. — [Steam MW5 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/784080/discussions/0/3780246651096705665) (forum)
- MW5 Clans weapon identity: a gauss rifle and a PPC-like weapon "feel very different just on how fast the projectile moves and drops". SRMs and LRMs have "very clear flight patterns to master". Fire-and-forget autocannon versus a laser "you must keep on target" versus a pulse laser "that splits the difference" make "a clearer set of build choices than ever before." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)
- Hawken: weapons use no ammo but build heat, and overheating locks out weapons for a short time. A preview says it encourages "being smart with weaponry rather than reckless". PCWorld wanted an option to disable the failsafe "even at the risk of destroying the mech". The 2018 console update retuned heat per mech. — [GamesBeat, Hawken open beta preview](https://gamesbeat.com/hawkens-open-beta-shows-a-thoughtfully-designed-mech-combat-game-preview/); [PCWorld E3 2012 hands-on](https://www.pcworld.com/article/465079/e3_2012_hands_on_with_hawken.html); [505 Games May 2018 update](https://support.505games.com/support/solutions/articles/150000147036-what-s-in-the-may-2018-update-for-hawken-on-ps4-and-xbox-one-)
- Armored Core VI: fire and plasma weapons build heat that can cause an "ACS anomaly". When that bar fills, "you will instantly be staggered, whether your main stagger bar is at 0 or not." — [Prima Games, ACS Anomaly](https://primagames.com/gaming/what-does-acs-anomaly-mean-in-armored-core-6) (via search snippet, page 403)

### Inferences
- The PCWorld Hawken quote and MWO's override show the **player-chosen risk** pattern: let the pilot override the safety and pay in internal damage. That's more "sim" than a hard lockout, and it creates dramatic choices. IRONFEUD has shutdowns and a coolant flush, so an override with structure damage would be a natural extension. The AI could use it based on personality (aggressive pilots override).
- Ghost heat is the cautionary tale: a hidden nonlinear penalty is hated. If IRONFEUD ever needs to curb alphas, it should do it through a **visible, physical mechanism**. Examples: recoil and convergence spread scaling with simultaneous shots, reactor draw shown on the HUD, or heat that spikes visibly. Not a silent tax.
- **Ambient heat per map** (desert hot, arctic cold, water wading as cooling) is cheap, sourced in lore across the genre, and gives terrain a heat role. It also creates per-class jobs: hot maps punish energy-heavy assaults, and water crossings help brawlers.
- Ammo explosions and limited ammo are what make ballistics and missiles a real tradeoff against energy. For a single-player campaign with persistent damage, ammo also becomes an upkeep or economy lever (see the campaign-layer research).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve MWO's current heat numbers (base cap, heat sink dissipation, the ghost-heat linkage window, which I recall as about 0.5 s, and the per-weapon multiplier table). nav-alpha's ghost heat page and the official wiki did not render.
- No sourced data on MW2 or MW4 heat scales, chain-fire defaults or ammo explosion odds. I found no reliable source on MW4's "ammo explosion on internal crit" behaviour or MW5's CASE rules.
- No MW5-specific confirmed coolant flush or map-temperature mechanics. The ambient-heat findings are from MWO and the Living Legends mod.

## 4. Lancemate and enemy AI in MW5 and MW4: praise and complaints

### Takeaway
Across MW4 and MW5, AI lancemates are described as "functional but comically inept with terrain". Typical failures: friendly fire, shooting into cliffs, getting stuck, ignoring minimum range, wrecking the objective. Patches over five-plus years mostly fixed specific bugs (navmesh, turret targeting, LRM fire rate) rather than changing the behavioural model. Critics forgive weak AI when they get precise control. They do not forgive AI that wastes the player's mission.

### Cited Findings
- MW5 Mercs: AI of both sides is "relatively functional but often comically inept at understanding the terrain". Allies "smash whatever you're supposed to defend or launch missiles into cliffsides". A lancemate left with only long-range weapons will keep "circle-strafing an opponent inside their minimum range". Enemies get stuck on terrain, forcing a hunt "for one last, tiny tank." — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercs](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)
- MW5 Clans (2024): "Your allies and the enemies' AI are still fairly weak", but better command tools compensate. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/). A user-review aggregator lists "AI allies are useless" among common complaints. — [Vaporlens, MW5 Clans](https://vaporlens.app/app/2000890/mech_warrior_5_clans)
- Common MW5 player complaints: friendly fire, allies walking into your line of fire, running into enemy lines, getting stuck on terrain. — [Vaporlens, MW5 Mercs](https://vaporlens.app/app/784080/mech_warrior_5_mercenaries); [Steam MW5 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/784080/discussions/0/3417684283225247486)
- Per a mod page comment (not official), a year-one update stopped AI friendly fire, leaving only players able to cause it. — [Steam Workshop comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/2498056614)
- MW5 patch 1.08 (March 2023): improved the rate at which friendly AI fires LRMs, fixed lancemates shooting at incoming DropShip turrets, and fixed unresponsive AI mechs. — [MP1st, MW5 update 1.08](https://mp1st.com/title-updates-and-patches/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-update-1-08-out-for-improvements-this-march-9)
- MW5 Chaos Reign Patch 1 (June 2026) improved lancemate AI to reduce chances of getting stuck off the navmesh. Navigation is still being patched in 2026. — [MW5 official news, Chaos Reign Patch 1](https://mw5mercs.com/news/2026/06/191-chaos-reign-patch-1) (via search snippet)
- MW4 Mercs: lancemates "follow your orders well", with a control system "very well designed once the basic commands are learned" — [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/mechwarrior-4-mercenaries/review); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/gaming/1854/mechwarrior_4_mercenaries_review/index.html). Contradicted by: "lancemate AI is non existent" — [EVGA forum review](https://forums.evga.com/Mechwarrior-Mercenaries-Quick-and-Dirty-Review-m3020647.aspx)
- MW5 Mercs: large mixed enemy groups (mechs, helicopters, tanks, turrets) can overwhelm the player. Enemy pressure comes from numbers, not cleverness. — [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)
- Missions as AI showcase: MW5 Clans has set pieces where "dozens of units for either side clashed amid the chaos of incoming artillery with aerospace fighters". On normal difficulty you must "figure out the best way to play each fight". Complaint: no mid-mission saves on 30-minute missions. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)

### Inferences
- The specific MW5 failure modes make a ready-made **regression test list** for IRONFEUD's headless AI. Each is measurable: no fire whose line of fire crosses a friendly; no weapon fired into terrain occluding the target; respect minimum range (move out or switch target when only long-range weapons remain); never damage the defend objective; stuck detection (no progress for N s → replan). IRONFEUD already uses identical `MechInput` and scan-only perception, which avoids the "cheating AI" critique. The gap the genre shows is terrain literacy.
- "Enemy pressure by numbers" is the genre default and the anti-pattern for IRONFEUD's goal #3. The opportunity is enemies that reposition, retreat damaged mechs, focus fire, and use cover. Critics never got that from MW5.
- AI that holds fire to avoid overheating is good fallible-pilot behaviour, but it reads as passivity. IRONFEUD should show the intent visibly (vents, a debug overlay "holding: heat").

### Gaps
- No GDC talk or developer post found on MW5 AI architecture (behaviour trees vs utility). No source on MW4 enemy AI design.
- Could not confirm officially whether MW5 AI friendly fire was removed or reduced.

## 5. What do long-time fans say newer games lost or gained versus MW2/MW4?

### Takeaway
Fans and critics credit the modern entries with better handling, physics, destruction and weapon identity. MW5 Clans is called "best-ever" combat. What they say was lost: hardware sim support (HOTAS and pedals), sensors and targeting depth, memorable mission framing (MW4's DropShip support, MW2's story beats), and physical attacks. It is mostly fidelity at the edges, not the core loop. The sandbox-versus-handcrafted split (MW5 Mercs vs Clans) is a live tradeoff, not a settled answer.

### Cited Findings
- GameRevolution: MW5 Mercs "feels like a step back from MechWarrior 4 in many ways" despite better graphics. Controls work much like MW4 and "Each mech feels vastly different from the next." Damage model: mechs are "too fragile and too strong at the same time": limbs and weapons go while the mech stays mobile, and the cockpit and torso are too armoured to hit reliably, so players shoot legs. Only five mission types, repeated with different environments and difficulty, which "feel like a grind." — [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)

### Inferences
- GameRevolution's "too fragile and too strong at once / everyone shoots legs" is a **damage-geometry lesson**. If the torso and cockpit are effectively immune, the optimal target becomes legs, which homogenises play. IRONFEUD's "legs → speed cap" plus fragile internals needs a headless check of which section kills come from. The goal is a spread, not a legging meta.
- **Mission framing** (support assets that fire, a real extraction under fire) costs little and was explicitly missed. IRONFEUD's support calls could include an extraction or dropship-cover phase.
- For IRONFEUD's campaign, MW5's two answers (procedural sandbox vs handcrafted linear) map onto its planned galaxy layer. Critics praised the sandbox for variety and the handcrafted missions for set pieces. A hybrid (procedural contracts plus authored set pieces) avoids both "grind" and "no replay."

## 6. Armored Core VI's contrasting answer (ACS stagger, assembly), Titanfall and HBS BattleTech, and what a weighty sim could borrow

### Takeaway
AC6 makes impact (posture) a second health bar that recovers over time. Filling it opens a short window where specific weapons deal bonus "direct hit" damage, and that turns loadouts into combos (build pressure, break, punish). Assembly is designed so that **legs define movement feel**, not just stats. HBS BattleTech's stability, knockdown and called-shot loop and Titanfall's doomed state are the genre's other proven "punish window" and "dramatic last stand" designs. All three can be ported to a weighty sim if the stagger is expressed physically (the gait loses balance, the torso is thrown off aim) rather than as an animation lock.

### Cited Findings
- AC6 ACS: taking many hits in a short window overloads the ACS (Attitude Control System), leaving the mech briefly vulnerable. Enemy impact sits as a yellow bar above health, and part of the meter stays filled even after you stop attacking. — [Yahoo/GamesRadar, "Mechiro" piece citing PlayStation Blog](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/sekiro-fans-dub-armored-core-205346189.html); [Destructoid, impact meter](https://destructoid.com/how-to-stagger-enemies-with-impact-meter-in-armored-core-6-ac6)
- Player ACS: when full, "a vulnerable state where you take more damage". It is mitigated by the Attitude Stability (resistance) and Attitude Recovery (recovery speed) stats. High-impact weapon classes: bazookas, grenade launchers, missile launchers. Shotguns and handguns are also cited. — [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/how-to-stagger-enemies-with-impact-meter-in-armored-core-6-ac6); [GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/guides/stagger-enemies-armored-core-6/)
- Direct Hit Adjustment is an "Extra damage multiplier to staggered enemies", per weapon. Impact is how much stagger the ACS can take before becoming immobile, and it "resets over time". A Steam player claims stagger duration depends on ACS weight, with lighter frames recovering faster (anecdotal). — [Prima Games, AC6 stat terms (via search snippet; page 403)](https://primagames.com/gaming/armored-core-6-all-detailed-ac-stat-and-part-spec-terms-explained); [Prima Games, ACS stagger tips](https://primagames.com/tips/armored-core-6-how-to-make-the-most-of-enemy-acs-stagger-in-ac6); [Steam AC6 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1888160/discussions/0/4036978698783149522/)
- Director Masaru Yamamura on stagger as a build tool: players can assign one weapon to build impact, one to trigger the stagger, and one to deal direct damage while the enemy is vulnerable, "coordinated attacks utilizing multiple weapons". Assembly "affects not only the mech's parameters, but also the play and feel as an action game, especially the leg components that determine the mech's movement." The team did not want to reduce "the breadth and complexity of the customization and assembly". Target Assist was added because of faster combat. — [PlayStation Blog interview with AC6 creators](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=383443); [OneMoreGame, AC6 interview on Target Assist](https://onemoregame.ph/?p=116859)
- Yamamura: the battle shares an "essence ... aggressive, speed change and action-oriented fighting" with Sekiro, built around slow and fast speed changes. He denied direct Sekiro elements. — [EGM, Miyazaki/Yamamura on AC6](https://egmnow.com/armored-core-vi-wont-have-soulsborne-type-gameplay-miyazaki-says/); [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/armored-core-6-wont-just-be-a-sci-fi-soulslike-according-to-miyazaki)
- HBS BattleTech (turn-based): evasion pips come from movement and each hit strips one. A mech with none left takes significantly more damage. Stability damage pushes a target to Unsteady (stripping evasion) and then a knockdown. Knockdowns or heat shutdowns enable called shots, which shift the hit-location distribution rather than guarantee it. Mods repeatedly retune called-shot multipliers and stability damage, and one replaces evasion so fast and small mechs are simply harder to hit. — [TechRaptor, 6 BattleTech tips](https://techraptor.net/content/6-battletech-tips); [Penny Arcade forum](https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/219284/battletech-mechwarrior-old-thread-like-the-carcass-of-a-cored-out-locust); [PC Gamer BattleTech guide](https://pcgamer.com/battletech-guide); [Nexus mod 131](https://nexusmods.com/battletech/mods/131) (community sources)
- PC Gamer contrasted HBS BattleTech's melee "featuring so gloriously" with its absence in MW5. — [PC Gamer, MW5 Mercs](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)
- Titanfall doomed state: once health and shields are gone, the titan enters a visibly charred, struggling "doomed" state. In Titanfall 1 the bar erodes over several seconds and the pilot must eject. In Titanfall 2 it no longer erodes, so a careful pilot can survive in doomed state "for the entire match". But a rodeo battery-pull or grenade in the exhaust, or a termination (execution), kills it instantly. Since Monarch's Reign, any doomed titan can be terminated regardless of shield. — [Titanfall wiki.gg, Doomed State](https://titanfall.wiki.gg/wiki/Doomed_State) (fan wiki)

It explains the large sequel changes (rodeo steals a battery, a cat-and-mouse game). — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024094/The-Evolution-of-Rodeo-in)

### Inferences
- **Stability as a physical meter, not a stun.** IRONFEUD has springs and footfall impulses, so it can implement an AC6/HBS-style stability budget natively. Heavy impacts (cannon, missiles, collisions, Death From Above) add impulse to the secondary-motion springs and drain a stability reserve. Low stability raises aim error (torso thrown off) and widens convergence. At zero, the gait solver must take a recovery step (stagger) or the mech falls (knockdown). That is a "punish window" grounded in physics, which keeps weight instead of turning it into an arcade lock. Recovery scaled by tonnage (heavier = harder to stagger, slower to recover) gives each class a distinct profile, which helps goal #4: lights stagger less often because hits rarely land, assaults resist stagger but take longer to recover.
- **Direct-hit-style combos map onto IRONFEUD's weapon roster.** Impact-heavy weapons (cannons, rocket pods) as setup and precise weapons (particle cannon, lasers) as punish rewards mixed loadouts and lance teamwork (one mech staggers, another punishes). That supports goal #3: the AI can coordinate visibly.
- **Called shots gated on target state** (HBS: called shots only on knocked-down or shut-down targets) is a strong, readable rule for when precision targeting of a section becomes possible.
- **Doomed state / last stand:** a "reactor critical or structure gone, mech still standing" phase gives drama and readable enemy state, with a clear decision (eject now vs fight on). It fits persistent pilot injury in the campaign (eject = pilot survives, mech salvage lost or damaged).
- **Assembly lesson:** AC6 makes legs the movement-defining part. IRONFEUD's two body models (biped vs digitigrade) could carry real movement differences (turn rate, slope access, stagger resistance) rather than only hit-sphere differences. That is a transferable way to make models distinct without stat bloat.
