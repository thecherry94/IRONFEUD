# Animation and physics for large walking machines: falls, knockdowns, hit reactions, melee, procedural/physics locomotion

Scope note: this covers techniques and shipped-game practice for making big walkers feel heavy when they get hit, fall, get up and fight in melee, read against IRONFEUD's current stack (gait phase, planted feet, analytic 2-bone IK, springs with footfall impulses, gyro torso, raycast footholds, 120 Hz fixed tick, headless `BattleSim` in pure C#). Two facts about the codebase checked this session shape the inferences. First, the mech rig is a C#-built node hierarchy with no `Skeleton3D`, `PhysicalBone3D` or `RigidBody3D` anywhere in `src/`. Second, `project.godot` already selects `3d/physics_engine="Jolt Physics"`.

Source quality warning: primary sources for shipped-game animation pipelines (Guerrilla, Respawn, FromSoftware, Piranha) are mostly GDC Vault videos with only short abstracts online. Several searches found only abstracts or press summaries. Claims that rest only on background knowledge are marked "unverified" and listed under Gaps, never under Cited Findings.

## 1. How shipped games animated big machines (authored vs procedural IK vs physics)

### Takeaway
Every shipped big-machine game I could source uses authored (keyframe or mocap) animation as the base. Procedural IK, physics and damage layers go on top, and dramatic events (embarks, executions, doomed states, staggers) are authored set pieces. No major title found ships a fully physics-driven giant walker. IRONFEUD's fully procedural base is unusual. Falls and melee will be the hardest places to stay procedural, and the industry answer there is "author a few key poses, then let procedural layers adapt them".

### Inferences
- The pattern across Titanfall, AC6 and HZD is that big hit consequences are discrete authored states (stagger, doomed, embark, execution) triggered by gameplay meters. Physical plausibility comes from layering, not simulation. For IRONFEUD the procedural equivalent is "discrete state + procedural pose generator", with no mocap clip involved.
- The MWO anecdote (knockdown removed for abuse) is a design warning that fits IRONFEUD's "never a pattern to farm" rule. Knockdowns need diminishing returns, or a post-recovery immunity window, so a light can't lock an assault down.
- Terrain clipping is the stated reason the community expects canned knockdowns to fail. IRONFEUD already has raycast foot placement and support-height smoothing, and those are exactly what lets a procedural fall or get-up adapt to slopes.

## 2. Active ragdolls and physics-based characters: what is practical for a hobby engine at 120 Hz

### Takeaway
There are three tiers. Tier 1 is keypose-plus-procedural with no rigid-body sim (Overgrowth, Rain World): cheap, robust, and already IRONFEUD's style. Tier 2 is a powered ragdoll with PD joint servos and a simple balance feedback rule (Euphoria-like; SIMBICON is the research minimum): feasible but tuning-heavy and fragile. Tier 3 is RL-trained physics controllers (DeepMimic, AMP): impractical for a hobby project with many chassis. For IRONFEUD the right call is Tier 1 for everything plus a short-lived reduced-model sim only for falls.

### Cited Findings
- David Rosen's GDC 2014 Animation Bootcamp talk "An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation" (Wolfire) shows how simple procedural techniques create interactive, fluid animation from very few keyframes, with examples from Overgrowth, Receiver and Black Shades. — [Game Developer video](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation); [GameAnim summary](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=15568); [Unity forum thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation-gdc-video-talk/538228)
- Even so, much of Overgrowth's animation is driven by keyed poses that are blended and modified procedurally. — [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2093086)
- Rain World: the procedural animation technique came first (2011). Creatures are multiple sprites "puppeted by real-time algorithms" rather than playing pre-made animations, and creature AI and animation are intertwined. — [Game Developer: Crafting the ecosystem of Rain World](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-); [Unity blog: procedural design in Rain World](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)
- Euphoria (NaturalMotion) is built on "Dynamic Motion Synthesis": interactive characters synthesised at runtime on the CPU, not canned animation. Demos showed characters keeping their footing on a swaying rope bridge, stumbling and grasping for stability. — [Digital Production, SIGGRAPH 2006](https://digitalproduction.com/2006/08/02/siggraph-euphoria-und-morpheme-ergaenzen-endorphin/); [Wikipedia: NaturalMotion](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/NaturalMotion)
- GTA IV was the first commercial Euphoria title, with pedestrians knocked off balance stumbling dynamically. Later Rockstar titles reportedly made reactions stiffer and shorter. A fan wiki claims GTA V limits Euphoria-driven characters to three at a time; this is a weak source, but it suggests per-character cost. — [gta.wiki Euphoria](https://gta.wiki/w/Euphoria)
- SIMBICON (Yin, Loken, van de Panne, SIGGRAPH 2007) controls a simulated biped with a finite state machine of target poses tracked by PD joint servos, plus a balance feedback rule on body position and velocity. It produces walking in all directions, running, skipping and hopping in real time, in 2D and 3D. Its known weakness is a hip-driven, marching-like gait. — [SIMBICON paper PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/Simbicon.htm)
- Follow-ups optimise SIMBICON's parameters and generalise it (Generalized Biped Walking Control, Coros/Beaudoin/van de Panne). — [SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=105848)
- DeepMimic (Peng et al., 2018, ACM TOG 37:143) uses RL to track reference motion clips with hand-designed rewards: precise but inflexible. AMP (Peng, Ma, Abbeel, Levine, Kanazawa 2021) replaces the tracking objective with an adversarial motion prior learned from unstructured clips, plus simple task rewards. DeepMimic tracks more precisely; AMP composes skills more flexibly. — [AMP arXiv 2104.02180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02180v2); [SIGGRAPH history: AMP](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=121260)

### Inferences
- SIMBICON is the practical upper bound for a hobby powered-ragdoll walker. It is literally a pose FSM plus PD servos plus one balance rule, which a robotics engineer can write in an afternoon. But it would replace, not augment, IRONFEUD's exact-IK locomotion, and SIMBICON gaits look marching-like. Not recommended for locomotion. Its PD-servo-to-target-pose idea is the right tool for the fall phase only.
- RL controllers (DeepMimic/AMP) need training infrastructure, reference motions, and retraining per chassis and body model. They conflict with the four-class, two-body-model roster and with the "no Godot types / deterministic" logic rule. Not practical.
- Euphoria's lesson for IRONFEUD is behavioural, not technical. Characters reacting with *intent* (grabbing, bracing, stepping to catch themselves) read as alive. IRONFEUD can get most of that from the AI layer choosing a recovery step and the IK layer executing it, with no physics sim needed.
- Cost at 120 Hz: a powered ragdoll of about 10-15 bodies per mech, 8 mechs, in Jolt is computationally trivial. The real cost is stability tuning (stiff PD gains at heavy masses), determinism (Jolt in Godot is not the headless `BattleSim`), and the logic/render split (see section 5).
- Gang Beasts, TABS, Exanima and Wobbledogs (all active-ragdoll titles) deliberately sell wobbliness and comedy. The heaviness goal argues against a visible powered-ragdoll look on a 12 m walker. Heavy objects should look *over-damped and slow*, not floppy.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were fetched for Exanima, Gang Beasts, TABS or Wobbledogs. Unverified: all use powered ragdolls (joint motors toward an animated or target pose plus upright torque on the torso). Wobbledogs additionally evolves body plans with procedural gait.
- No GDC/NaturalMotion technical talk on Euphoria's internals was found. Its specific balance algorithm is undocumented publicly as far as these searches show.
- Rosen's talk specifics (e.g. how few keyframes the run cycle uses, and how the get-up and ragdoll blend work) were not verified from the video. Unverified recollection: Overgrowth's character blends to ragdoll on big hits and blends back to a keyframed get-up, and its walk/run uses very few poses interpolated by gait phase with spring-smoothed transitions.

## 3. Falls and getting up: deciding WHEN a mech falls, and animating it procedurally

### Takeaway
Use the robotics balance criterion, capture point on a linear inverted pendulum, as the fall trigger, and judge it against where the gait *can* step, not against the current support polygon. On a fall, hand the pose to a short reduced-model simulation (pelvis, torso, and maybe thigh bodies with PD toward a "brace" pose). Once at rest, run a keyframed get-up as a sequence of key poses solved through the existing IK with planted contacts.

### Cited Findings
- Capture point (Pratt, Carff, Drakunov, Goswami, Humanoids 2006): a point on the ground where a biped can step to come to a stop without another step. The set of such points is the capture region. Recovery uses stepping plus internal angular momentum. — [Capture Point: A Step toward Humanoid Push Recovery](https://cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-PrattCDG2006.html)
- Decision rule from the capture-point patents: if the instantaneous capture point lies inside the convex hull of the stance foot, the robot recovers without stepping; otherwise it must step. The capture point is computed from a Linear Inverted Pendulum Plus Flywheel model, and an experimentally determined error margin can be added for model mismatch. — [US7949430B2 Determination of foot placement for humanoid push recovery](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7949430); [US8195332B2 Learning capture points](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8195332)
- Later work combines ankle, hip and stepping strategies in MPC, and notes stepping is not always possible (limited contact surfaces). This matters for terrain-blocked recovery steps. — [Shafiee-Ashtiani et al., arXiv 1612.08034](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.08034)
- Capture-point feedback works on a *position-controlled* humanoid without torque sensors (SURENA-Mini). This is analogous to IRONFEUD's kinematic, IK-driven legs. — [arXiv 1710.10598](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1710.10598)
- SIMBICON's balance feedback modifies the swing-hip target from body position and velocity, a heuristic form of foot placement that keeps a PD-driven biped up. — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)

### Inferences
- The LIPM capture point is ξ = x_com + v_com/ω with ω = √(g/z_com). For a mech with its CoM about 7 m up, ω ≈ √(9.81/7) ≈ 1.18 rad/s, a time constant of about 0.85 s. At heavy speed (60 km/h ≈ 16.7 m/s), ξ sits about 14 m ahead of the CoM. A running mech is therefore *always* "falling" by the naive inside-the-polygon test; this is the "prototype numbers don't survive genre speeds" trap again. The usable criterion is whether ξ, after an impulse, is still reachable by the next planned foothold within leg reach and swing time. If not, the mech stumbles (an extra, shortened step toward ξ, executed by the existing gait/foothold code). If it is not reachable even with a max-length emergency step, the mech falls.
- This gives a physically grounded "stability budget" without an arbitrary meter. Incoming impulse J changes v_com by J/m. Heavier mechs move ξ less per hit, tall mechs have a slower ω (more time to step), and lights have short legs but fast cadence. Fall direction is the direction of ξ's escape. A stagger is graded by how far ξ ends up outside the reachable region. This ties falls to class identity naturally: mass, height and leg reach are already chassis data.
- Leg damage (already a speed cap) should shrink the reachable-step region, which makes a damaged mech fall more easily. That is good emergent consequence and readable intent.
- Terrain: if the capture step would land on a blocked or steep foothold (the foothold selector already rejects these), the mech falls. This is the MPC paper's "stepping not always possible" case, and it turns cliffs and rubble into knockdown terrain, a terrain-use tactic for the AI.
- Fall animation, a recommended reduced model: when the fall triggers, freeze the gait and spawn a pure-C# mini rigid-body sim in `IronFeud.Logic`. The pelvis is a box with the full mass, and the torso is a second body on a 2-DoF joint. Feet are point contacts that stay pinned while friction holds, then slide or lift. Pivot about the support edge toward ξ, with PD torques pulling the torso and arms toward a "brace" target pose. The visual legs and arms still come from the analytic IK, driven by targets taken from the mini-sim (knee to ground, hand to ground). That keeps "exact IK, cheated inputs" and keeps the headless `BattleSim` authoritative: hit spheres, the downed position and the footprint for collision are known without Godot.
- Impact: ground contact of the pelvis or torso fires big impulses into the existing secondary springs plus camera trauma, dust and sound. That is the same "weight comes from impulses" pipeline, scaled by impact speed, like the jump-jet touchdown code already does.
- Get-up: author 3-5 key poses per body model (prone or supine, to a knee-and-hand push, to a kneel, to a stand) as *contact targets* (which limbs are planted where, pelvis height and pitch), not as joint angles. Interpolate them on an eased timeline and solve each frame with the existing 2-bone IK against raycast ground. This is the Overgrowth "few keyframes + procedural" approach, and it adapts to slopes for free. Make get-up slow (several seconds for assaults) and interruptible by further hits; that is the gameplay cost of a fall.
- Fragility risks: (1) penetration and tunneling of a 40-100 t body at 120 Hz if contacts are stiff (use soft penalty contacts or position-based projection, not hard impulses). (2) The fall sim must be deterministic for auto-resolve calibration. (3) Downed mechs need hit spheres re-mapped from the lying pose.

### Gaps
- No source found on how any shipped mech game decides falls physically. BattleTech tabletop and HBS use dice and stability thresholds, AC6 a meter. No sourced example of a game using capture point to trigger falls; this appears to be novel for games, as far as these searches show.
- No sourced write-up of a procedural (non-mocap) get-up for a large creature. The Overgrowth get-up recollection is unverified.

## 4. Hit reactions and stagger as physics; stability-budget models

### Takeaway
Do hit reactions additively. Each hit is an impulse that (a) kicks the secondary-motion springs (local flinch: torso twist and pitch, arm swing) and (b) shifts the CoM velocity, which moves the capture point (global balance consequence). Express the "stability budget" of AC6 and BattleTech as capture-point margin plus a slowly recovering stance stiffness, so stagger is emergent, class-dependent and readable instead of an arbitrary bar.

### Cited Findings
- AC6 models stagger as an accumulating per-target gauge filled by weapon "Impact". Standing still doubles your exposure (half of impact accumulates when stationary versus less when boosting). Melee, shotguns and explosives are the high-impact tools, and a staggered target takes guaranteed direct hits. — [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/ac6-illegal-entry-how-to); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230825-armoredcore-vi-fires-of-rubicon-review); [pixiv](https://dic.pixiv.net/history/ACVI/5977255)
- Titanfall's titans show a damage state through stumbling and shaking in the doomed state. — [Prima Games eGuide (403, search snippet)](https://primagames.com/eguides/titanfall-eguide/behind-the-scenes/animation)
- Euphoria-driven characters react to being knocked with dynamic stumbling (GTA IV), which is the gold standard for "the hit changed my balance". — [gta.wiki Euphoria](https://gta.wiki/w/Euphoria)
- Capture-point theory supplies the strategies a pushed biped uses in order of severity: ankle (CoP shift within the foot), hip/flywheel (angular momentum), then stepping. — [Pratt et al. 2006](https://cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-PrattCDG2006.html); [arXiv 1612.08034](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.08034)

### Inferences
- These map directly onto IRONFEUD layers. **Ankle strategy** is the small hit: spring impulse only, and the gyro torso recovers. **Hip strategy** is the medium hit: torso and pelvis counter-rotation (gyro briefly saturates, aim is thrown off, which is gameplay), feet stay planted. **Stepping strategy** is the large hit: the gait inserts a stumble step toward the new capture point and the cadence breaks. **Fall** is the capture point beyond the reachable region. That is a 4-level graded reaction from one continuous quantity, and it is readable because each level looks different.
- An AC6-style "accumulation" can be expressed physically as stance stiffness or gyro authority that drops with recent hits and recovers over time. Each hit lowers the gyro's max correction torque, so successive hits push the torso and ξ further. Standing still and bracing could *raise* stiffness. That inverts AC6's stationary penalty, which may suit heavy assaults as a "brace" job, though it is a design choice. A heat-shutdown or a damaged gyro component (critical effect) could zero it.
- Per-weapon "impact" should be the actual momentum transfer (projectile mass × velocity, explosive impulse), clamped by gameplay scalars. Then cannons and missiles stagger, lasers barely do, and melee does the most. That matches AC6's tiering and gives lights (fast, low-impact weapons) and heavies distinct roles.
- Anti-farm safeguards (CLAUDE.md principle 3): add diminishing returns on repeated knockdowns, brief post-get-up stability boost, and AI awareness of its own ξ margin (a cautious pilot slows before cresting rubble when under heavy fire).
- Directional reaction: apply the impulse at the hit sphere's world position. The torque about the CoM gives torso twist for an arm or shoulder hit and a pitch-back for a centre hit, with no per-direction authored clip.

### Gaps
- No official FromSoftware formula for ACS (decay rate, recovery, weight dependence) found. All AC6 numbers come from guides and press.
- HBS BattleTech stability rules were not sourced (see section 1 Gaps).

## 5. Melee for heavy mechs: making strikes read as massive

### Takeaway
Heaviness in melee comes from timing (long anticipation, fast but not instant strike, hitstop on contact, long recovery/follow-through), momentum consequences (the striker's body actually moves and must rebalance, and the target's capture point is pushed), and feedback (camera trauma, sound, debris). Use IK-driven strikes, with hand/foot targets on authored Bezier paths relative to the target, plus a lunge-assist that steers the whole body through the normal `MechInput` locomotion, so AI and player share it. Detect hits with swept spheres on the striking limb against the existing hit spheres.

### Cited Findings
- Hitstop or hitlag is a short freeze for both attacker and victim on contact, there "to give the player a sense of the attack hitting as well as the weight of the attack". Capcom's example scales it by strength: light/medium/heavy = 8/12/16 frames (at 60 fps). Multi-hit moves shorten it so they don't feel too heavy, and projectiles cause no attacker hitstop. — [Capcom SFV column](https://game.capcom.com/cfn/sfv/column/131545)
- KOF XIII lists 7 frames of hitstop for light attacks and 11 for heavy. — [SRK wiki KOF XIII](https://srk.shib.live/w/The_King_of_Fighters_XIII/Character_States/Hitstun_Blockstun_state)
- Sakurai (Smash Bros.) warns that stopping on every hit slows the game overall. Smash scales hitlag with damage dealt. — [Source Gaming translation of Sakurai's Famitsu column](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/); [SmashWiki Hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)
- A taxonomy and critique of hitstop and its variants (hitfreeze, hitpause, hitshake) is given in [critpoints.net](https://critpoints.net/2017/05/17/hitstophitfreezehitlaghitpausehitshit/). Some players argue hitstop makes weapons feel like they hit things, others that it harms flow. — [Steam: Monster Hunter Wilds discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2246340/discussions/0/4630357016599074272)
- A common implementation is a brief time-scale drop to zero then restore, with a capped max freeze. — [uhiyama-lab Unity game-feel primer](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/unity/unity-game-feel-hit-feedback/)
- AC6 counts melee among the highest-impact (stagger) tools. — [WePC](https://www.wepc.com/gaming/armored-core-6-stagger-enemies/)

### Inferences
- **Hitstop in a 120 Hz deterministic sim**: don't change `Engine.TimeScale` globally (it breaks the AI-vs-AI and headless parity and freezes everyone). Instead apply a *local* hitstop. Freeze or slow only the two participants' limb-target timelines (the striking arm's path parameter and the victim's spring integration) for about 60-130 ms, scaled by impulse. Use a camera-only time dip for the player-involved hit. On a 120 Hz tick, Capcom's 8-16 frames at 60 fps is 16-32 ticks. For giant-scale "heaviness", the lower end plus a long follow-through probably reads better than long freezes, but this needs testing.
- **Anticipation, strike, recovery** for a 12 m mech: about 0.6-1.2 s wind-up (torso twist away, arm draw-back, weight shift onto the back foot, visible as AI intent, which serves principle 3's readability), a 0.15-0.3 s strike, and a 1-2 s recovery during which the striker is vulnerable. These are design starting points, not sourced numbers. The long wind-up is also the counterplay window for the target to twist away or step back.
- **IK-driven strike**: the hand/fist target follows a cubic path in the torso frame (draw-back point, contact point at the target's predicted hit-sphere position, follow-through point past it). The existing analytic arm IK solves it, so it adapts to target height and distance automatically. The torso twist and pelvis weight shift are driven by the *same* path (twist leads the hand by about 0.1 s for a whip feel). Kicks: the gait yields one leg as a "swing" with a strike target, and the stance leg must hold the capture point, so a kick at speed can trip the kicker. That is realistic risk and an emergent anti-spam measure.
- **Momentum transfer**: on contact compute the exchanged impulse from limb effective mass and closing speed, capped. Apply it to the victim's springs, its CoM velocity (capture point) and the hit-section damage. Apply the reaction to the striker too: a punch into an assault staggers a light more than the assault. A charge or ram uses whole-body momentum m·v, so an assault ramming at 40 km/h is about 4× a light's momentum at 130 km/h for 4-5× the mass. That gives the class difference without special-casing.
- **Lunge assistance**: soft target lock that steers the striker's heading and throttle *through `MechInput`*, the same channel the AI uses (principle 2). Clamp it to the chassis turn rate and acceleration, and "magnetise" only within a cone and range. The arm IK handles the last metre of error. No positional snapping: snapping is what makes melee look weightless.
- **Collision and hit detection**: sweep a sphere (or capsule) along the fist or foot path *between ticks* against the target's existing hit spheres in `BattleSim`. At 120 Hz and about 15-25 m/s fist speed, that is 0.13-0.2 m per tick, small relative to hit-sphere radii, so a simple sphere-sphere test per tick at the current and previous positions is enough. Resolve one hit per swing per target (hit-once list), and add a "clank" deflection if the fist meets cover geometry (ArenaMap rays) first.
- **Physical weapons** (blades, hammers, shields): treat them as rigid extensions of the hand target with their own mass. Heavier weapons mean slower path speed and larger impulse. A shield is a hit sphere attached to the arm that intercepts projectiles, which ties into the existing facing-armour model.
- **Feedback stack per hit**: local hitstop, camera trauma scaled by impulse and distance, a spring impulse on the victim *and* the striker, a sparks/debris particle, a low-frequency thump, and a servo whine spike (ServoSynth already keys off joint velocity, so the sudden stop will produce a natural audio cue).

### Gaps

## 6. Godot 4 specifics: physical bones, Jolt, SkeletonModifier3D, limits

### Takeaway
Godot 4.4+ ships Jolt built in (IRONFEUD already uses it), and `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` (a `SkeletonModifier3D`) can simulate selected bones. But it requires a `Skeleton3D`, which IRONFEUD does not use, and community reports call active ragdolls on Jolt fiddly and unstable. Physics-in-Godot also lives outside the headless `BattleSim`. Recommendation: do falls in pure C# (`IronFeud.Logic`) and keep Godot physics, if used at all, for cosmetic debris and detached parts.

### Cited Findings
- Godot 4.4 integrated Jolt Physics as a built-in option, labelled experimental, not the default, and missing some Godot Physics features. It is enabled via `physics/3d/physics_engine`. Making Jolt the default is a stated future plan. — [GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/godot-4-4-gets-native-jolt-physics-support/); [DevClass](https://devclass.com/2025/03/05/godot-4-4-released-open-source-game-engine-adds-jolt-physics-net-8-and-more/); [heise](https://heise.de/-10305042)
- `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` (inherits `SkeletonModifier3D`) is the parent of `PhysicalBone3D` nodes and applies their simulation to a `Skeleton3D`. Its methods are `physical_bones_start_simulation(bones = [])` (a subset of named bones gives a partial ragdoll), `physical_bones_stop_simulation()`, `is_simulating_physics()` and collision-exception helpers. — [Godot docs: PhysicalBoneSimulator3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_physicalbonesimulator3d.html)
- The recommended setup makes `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` a child of `Skeleton3D`, and the `SkeletonModifier3D` `active` flag controls whether it contributes to the pose. Older setups used a now-deprecated internal simulator. — [Godot 4.3 docs: Skeleton3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.3/classes/class_skeleton3d.html)
- Community reports: the popular Godot 4.0 active-ragdoll tutorial breaks on 4.4.1 because `get_bone_global_pose()` returns the pre-modifier pose. Jolt implements spring constraints natively. Users report instability with Jolt's heavier bodies and odd behaviour after impacts, and further problems on 4.6 with both engines (unresolved). — [Forum: can't create active ragdoll](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/cant-create-active-ragdoll/113826); [Forum: active ragdolls with Jolt, is it possible?](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/active-ragdolls-in-godot-jolt-is-it-even-possible/117047); [Forum: Active Ragdoll Godot 4.6](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/active-ragdoll-godot-4-6/140387)
- An MIT-licensed "Active Ragdoll" addon (Godot 4.4) claims humanoid support on Jolt or Godot Physics, with puppet and hit-reaction features (not verified). — [Godot Asset Library](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/5575)

### Inferences
- IRONFEUD has no `Skeleton3D` (checked: no `Skeleton3D`, `PhysicalBone3D` or `RigidBody3D` in `src/`), so `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` would require re-rigging into a skeleton or hand-building `RigidBody3D` + `Generic6DOFJoint3D` chains on the node hierarchy. Both put authoritative fall state into Godot, breaking the CLAUDE.md rule that gameplay math lives in Godot-free C# and that headless `BattleSim` and auto-resolve calibration see the same physics.
- Recommended split: the fall/get-up mini-sim (2-4 bodies, PD toward brace pose, penalty ground contacts against the ArenaMap heightfield) goes in `IronFeud.Logic/Mech/`, deterministic and unit-testable, emitting limb contact targets for the existing IK. Godot/Jolt is used only for cosmetic, non-authoritative things: shed armour plates, a severed arm tumbling, debris from a melee hit. Hand those to `RigidBody3D` with initial velocity from the logic and never read back.
- Performance and stability: at 120 Hz a few-body custom integrator (semi-implicit Euler like the existing `Spring`, or a small Featherstone or position-based solver) is cheap. The main risk is stiff contact with 40-100 t masses. Use critically damped penalty springs sized by mass, or a PBD projection step, to avoid jitter. Measure with a diagnostic test before tuning ("measure before fixing").
- Jolt being "experimental" in 4.4 matters less here because it would only drive cosmetics. Note that 4.7.2 (the pinned version) may have changed its status. Verify in the 4.7 docs before relying on any Jolt-specific joint feature.
