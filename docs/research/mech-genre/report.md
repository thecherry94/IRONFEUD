# Constraint, Not Tonnage, Gives Mechs Soul

> Research report, 2026-11-02 (sources in `notes/`). Name check after writing: "Pavise" (a BattleTech battle
> station) and "Hauberk" (our current medium body, also an existing BattleTech mech) are taken and must be replaced
> under the original-IP rule; the other proposed names had no BattleTech wiki page.

The heart of the mech-sim genre is the feeling of being a pilot managing the limits of a huge machine whose mass you can feel, not the power of the machine. Across MechWarrior 2 through MW5: Clans, critics praise the same things. Throttle and legs are separate from torso aim. You twist to show your good armour. Heat is a running budget. Damage is tracked by section, and you lose weapons with the limbs that carry them. Mount height matters against cover. Scale shows when a big machine walks through cover that a small one needs. The modern games keep failing in the same places: physical contact (melee, collisions, knockdowns), sensors the player can't read, lancemates who waste the mission, and a campaign that funnels everyone into assaults. IRONFEUD already has most of the "sim heart" (twist, facing armour, heat with shutdowns, sectioned internals, signature by size, lance scans), and its procedural locomotion lets it do something the franchise never did: physical stagger and falls that come from balance, not canned clips. The next step that unblocks the most is a data refactor that makes chassis real body plans instead of scaled copies. After that, by order of leverage: mission objectives that make speed and information *win conditions*, a stability and impact model, ECM that degrades rather than blocks, intent-level lancemate orders, and recorded material in the audio. Every recommendation below respects the hard rules. AI uses the same inputs, every class stays viable, the weaker option gets the buff, and the headless matrix is the judge.

## Felt mass and managed limits are the genre's heart

Reviewers name the same core again and again. PC Gamer's MW5: Mercenaries review calls "throttle and orientation control" the key to its simulator feel. It says twisting "to strafe targets and show strong armor to the enemy is key", and it notes that high lasers "shoot over a wall" while low arm weapons "explode the cover you were using" ([PC Gamer, MW5 Mercenaries](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)). The MW5: Clans review repeats the list: facing, throttle, the "physical placement of its guns", and "managing your mech's heat output so you don't shut down". It credits Clans with the best handling in the series because of "a real sense of weight to how machines accelerate and decelerate", while calling its footstep and explosion sound "anemic" ([PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)). A 2026 MW2 retrospective sums up the old games' weight as "**Every shot has a cost. Every mistake leaves a mark**", and argues that simplifying movement and systems would have destroyed "the qualities that made it distinctive" ([Generation Amiga](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/07/25/mechwarrior-2-retrospective-review-why-it-became-a-pc-gaming-milestone/)). Scale comes from destruction more than from any number: in Clans a 100 t assault "plows through entire buildings a smaller mech uses as cover" ([PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)).

Fans say the modern games lost fidelity at the edges, not in the core loop: sensor depth, memorable mission framing (MW4's landed dropships that fired back), and physical attacks ([PC Gamer, MW5 Mercenaries](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)). GameRevolution names a damage-model trap: mechs are "too fragile and too strong at the same time". Limbs pop off easily while the centre torso is too tough to hit reliably, so **everyone shoots legs** ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)). Armored Core VI and Titanfall add two ideas worth borrowing. In AC6 the legs, not the stat sheet, define movement feel ([PlayStation Blog interview](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=383443)), and impact works as a second health bar that opens a punish window ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/how-to-stagger-enemies-with-impact-meter-in-armored-core-6-ac6)). Titanfall's "doomed" state gives a readable last stand ([Titanfall wiki.gg](https://titanfall.wiki.gg/wiki/Doomed_State)).

## Chassis identity lives in geometry, handling and jobs, not in scale

The clearest body-plan system in the genre is Armored Core VI's leg types. The biped is the all-rounder. The reverse joint is a light jumper with low load, defence and stagger resistance. The tetrapod is a stable platform that absorbs recoil while hovering. The tank has the most armour and load but struggles in the air ([Fextralife AC6 Legs](https://armoredcore6.wiki.fextralife.com/Legs)). The numbers make the trade explicit. Attitude stability runs from **530 on a reverse joint to 1,630 on a tank (about 3×)**. Reverse joints jump roughly 3-4× farther than bipeds (386 vs 83-103 distance) ([Armored Core Wiki KASUAR/42Z](https://armoredcore.fandom.com/wiki/KASUAR/42Z), [06-041 MIND ALPHA](https://armoredcore.fandom.com/wiki/06-041_MIND_ALPHA)). When reverse joints underperformed, the developer **buffed their weakness instead of nerfing bipeds** ([Steam AC6 patch discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1888160/discussions/0/4200238454189760494)). That is IRONFEUD's own rule.

In MechWarrior Online, much of a chassis' value came from geometry independent of its stats. What counted was hardpoint height (to peek over cover), a narrow centre torso, side torsos covered by arms big enough to soak hits, and the overall profile. Players rank hardpoint location and count above everything else ([MWO forums, ideal geometry](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/229826-what-is-ideal-mech-geometry/), [Best Hitboxes](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/262240-best-hitboxes/)). PGI's 2015 rebalance rated each variant on "loadout, agility, and geometric layout" ([MWO forums, Mech Rebalance And PTS](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/208441-mech-rebalance-and-pts/)). Aimed fire makes geometry decisive: the tabletop's random hit locations no longer spread damage, so "**the incentive to boat is massive**" ([Sarna News](https://www.sarna.net/news/the-challenge-with-balanace-in-mechwarrior-online/amp/)). IRONFEUD has already met this effect: its headless data showed Sicklefoot's smaller, lower hit spheres decided a matchup. So hit-volume layout (centre-torso width, arm-sphere size, hardpoint height above the hip, cockpit height over total height, frontal vs side cross-section) should be budgeted per chassis like a stat and pinned by tests. Hitboxes must stay on the visible parts. MWO's silent hitbox edits are a standing community grievance ([Taro's Hitbox Repository](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/216117-taros-hitbox-repository/)), and IRONFEUD's "the AI only perceives what a pilot could see" rule implies that what you see is what you hit.

Handling should be a property of the chassis inside a class band, decoupled from top speed. In 2017 PGI found that "too much was gained by upgrading Engines". It moved agility to the chassis, "roughly equal across tonnage lines", with per-chassis offsets tuned against peers in the same bracket ([MWO forums, 2017 PGI post](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/245742-new-skill-tree-changes-as-well-as-mech-mobility-decuppled-from-engine/)). Datamined MW5 files (341 variants) give the shape of a class ladder. **Torso yaw rate falls roughly as tons^-0.75, from about 225°/s at 20 t to 67.5°/s at 100 t**. Arm speed is 2.5× torso yaw, torso pitch 0.625× yaw, and turn rate about 0.8× yaw. Twist *bounds* shrink too, from ±110-130° on lights to ±62.5° at 85 t ([Career200/MW5-Mech-info](https://github.com/Career200/MW5-Mech-info), [MW5-mechlab](https://github.com/Career200/MW5-mechlab), derived). So a light out-twists an assault by roughly 2.2-3.3×, and that is the circling window that keeps lights alive next to assaults. MWO's 2015 test server applied the same logic to sensors, with ranges of about 900 m for lights down to 500-650 m for assaults ([MWO PTS Phase 2](https://mwomercs.com/news/2015/10/1370-mech-rebalance-pts-phase-2)).

Size needs its own rule. Mass scales with volume, so a mech 4× heavier at the same density is only ∛4 ≈ **1.6× taller**, an inference from the cube law, not sourced industry practice. Today IRONFEUD scales one body by 0.72/1.0/1.15/1.3 per class (`MechChassis.Scaled`, `src/IronFeud.Logic/Mech/MechChassis.cs`). That ties apparent size, hit area and proportions together. Giving each class its own proportions frees the hit area to be tuned per chassis. Readability then follows Valve's documented recipe: a read hierarchy of team (colour), then class (silhouette and proportions), then weapon (highest contrast at chest level). Designs are validated in pure silhouette, high-frequency detail is left out, and value runs from dark feet to a light chest ([Valve GDC 2008 slides](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf), [Mitchell et al., NPAR 2007](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall07/cos597B/papers/mitchell-team-fortress.pdf)). Dark legs have a bonus for IRONFEUD: they keep the eye on the torso, where the procedural weight reads best. Titanfall 2 shows the economical way to build a roster: six titans share three frames, two per frame, and kits make the difference ([Wikipedia, Titanfall 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanfall_2)). One rig can therefore carry two or three chassis that differ in shoulders, head, mounts and kit.

### Coordinator's visual study: five recurring body plans (coordinator's own observation, not sourced)

The coordinator studied about 95 reference line-art images of the franchise roster directly, only to extract generic patterns. Five body plans recur. The most common is a humanoid whose arms carry the weapons, found in every class. Next come a reverse-knee, bird-legged walker whose torso is a single cockpit pod; a squat brawler with the cockpit recessed between raised shoulders; a top-heavy assault with a wide chest and huge feet; and a hunched design where "the torso is the cockpit". Weapon placement falls into families: box launchers on the shoulders, long barrels mounted along the shoulders, fixed torso mounts versus free-aiming arm mounts, lopsided builds with one big-gun arm and a smaller other arm, and hand actuators or fists on brawlers. Class reads come from proportions. Lights have long thin legs and small torsos and are often armless, with weapon pods on the torso. Assaults have big masses, a shoulder line above the head, stubby proportions and skull-like faces. These are genre vocabulary, free to use as families. The legal danger, covered in the IP section, sits in *specific combinations* and faces, not in the families.

## No single loadout wins when each weapon family hits a different wall

Mechlabs have moved steadily toward letting the chassis decide the build. MW2 and MW3 were free-form and converged on a few optimal builds. MW4 typed its slots. MWO separated hardpoint type from slot count. MW5 typed and sized hardpoints ([Steam MW5 thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/784080/discussions/0/4337609830360929525), [backloggd review](https://backloggd.com/u/Cappn/likes/)). Loosen the restrictions and builds collapse. In MW5: Clans, freely configurable omnimechs produced the thread title "Every Single Meta Build: LASERBOAT", and one poster argued that Mercenaries' variety came *only* from hardpoint and slot limits ([Steam MW5: Clans](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2000890/discussions/0/4629230582747973584/)). Armored Core VI reaches identity without typed hardpoints. It uses several **orthogonal budgets** (leg load, arm load with a soft tracking penalty, generator output, energy capacity, stagger stability), each binding on a different part ([Armored Core 6 wiki: Stats](https://armoredcore6.wiki.fextralife.com/Stats)).

The genre's recurring dominant pattern is **pinpoint damage, at range, from behind cover**. MWO's "poptart" jumped up, fired PPCs plus a gauss rifle, and dropped back ([MWO forums](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/113540-sniperpoptart-problems-mechwarrior-3-has-your-solution)). When gauss was taxed, the meta moved to PPC+AC5 because the two had "very similar projectile speed" and fused into a single pinpoint volley ([MWO forums](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/164805-noob-after-a-definition/TimePeriod+)). Fixes that taxed the symptom invisibly were hated. Ghost heat made heat "grow exponentially" past a per-weapon volley limit ([MWO forums, Heat Scale](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/126651-heat-scale-and-general-update/)). Players called it "not entirely transparent and … seemingly arbitrary", and community simulators exist just to predict it ([MechSpecs](https://mechspecs.com/posts/82197/), [nav-alpha](https://mwo.nav-alpha.com/equipment/ghostheat)). The visible fix, screen and crosshair shake while jump jets fire, was readable ([patch 1.2.226](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/120882-patch-notes-12226-04-jun-2013)). In single-player, MW5's laser boats show the other root cause: **the weapon with no logistics cost wins**. Lasers have no ammo, no spread and a short cooldown, and missiles carry "high heat and weight requirements" ([Steam MW5: Clans](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2000890/discussions/0/4629230582747973584/)). HBS BattleTech solved its weak missiles with a *role* rather than damage. Missiles carry high stability damage that leads to knockdowns and then called shots, and players built destabiliser-plus-finisher lances around that ([Steam BattleTech](https://steamcommunity.com/app/637090/discussions/0/1697175413670359725), [Nexus guide](https://www.nexusmods.com/battletech/mods/233)). AC6's patch 1.07 shows how a long-lived game keeps variety: it was **mostly buffs** to underused weapons and frames ([Bandai Namco EU, patch 1.07](https://en.bandainamcoent.eu/armored-core/news/armored-core-vi-fires-of-rubicon-patch-notes-107)).

Theory backs the same moves. Sirlin defines balance as many viable options at high skill, calls a strictly dominant move the worst flaw a game can have, and wants counters three layers deep. He judges balance globally rather than skirmish by skirmish, and wants options that differ in *effect*, not just damage, speed and cost. His MvC2 example has 54 characters and about 10 viable ([Sirlin Part 2](https://sirlin.net/articles/balancing-multiplayer-games-part-2-viable-options)). In single-player the counters can only be soft ([critpoints.net](https://critpoints.net/2017/02/page/3)). Translated to IRONFEUD, that means several things. Use semi-fixed mounts per chassis (type, size, location, arc and height) rather than a free mechlab. Give each weapon family its own wall: heat for energy weapons, ammo and tonnage for ballistics and missiles, impact and stability as a separate axis. Keep projectile speeds of different families out of each other's band. Let mixed-mount volleys spread across sections through convergence geometry rather than a hidden tax. If a volley tax is ever needed, show it before the shot. Two IRONFEUD-specific checks follow. First, tabletop heat is graded rather than a cliff: movement penalties start at 17% of shutdown, to-hit penalties at 27%, shutdown risk at 47% ([MegaMek messages.properties](https://github.com/MegaMek/megamek/blob/main/megamek/resources/megamek/client/messages.properties), derived). IRONFEUD's 1.0 gun lock / 1.2 shutdown would gain a "running hot" band with soft penalties from about 0.5-0.7. Second, run a headless report of which section kills come from, so a legging meta can't form unnoticed.

Quirks deserve their own warning. MWO first used quirks to fix bad chassis. Stacked percentage buffs produced "an ever-growing disparity between a standard 'Mech chassis and the min/max end result" ([MWO forums](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/208441-mech-rebalance-and-pts/)). PGI then wiped them, folded mobility into baselines, and from 2021 used quirks "on diversity and flavor" rather than primary balance ([MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/2022/04/22/mechwarrior-onlines-latest-patch-adjusts-weapons-and-equipment-makes-a-quirk-pass-adds-shiny-mechs/)). BattleTech's design quirks are the better pattern: small, binary, situational traits that pair a positive with a negative. Examples are Overhead or Low-Mounted Arms (which interact with cover), Cowl, Narrow/Low Profile vs Oversized, Stable vs Unbalanced, Extended Torso Twist, and campaign quirks such as Rugged, Compact (half transport space), Easy or Difficult to Maintain, and Cramped Cockpit ([Sarna, List of Design Quirks](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/List_of_Design_Quirks)). The rule for IRONFEUD: each chassis gets at least one positive and one negative quirk. Quirks are tied to a mount location or a behaviour ("left arm has wider yaw"), never to a weapon family ("+25% laser range"), because the latter tells the player exactly what to mount. Logistics quirks push lights and mediums through the campaign without touching battle stats. The AI can read quirks, which turns them into readable behaviour. Utility equipment follows the same logic. MWO's ECM became mandatory because it was cheap, always useful and multi-role, and because "its best counter is itself" ([MWO forums](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/110187-the-state-of-guardian-ecm)). Point defense only stays a real choice if missile-heavy enemy compositions appear *sometimes*.

## Physical contact is the hole every MechWarrior left open, and IRONFEUD can fill it

MW5: Mercenaries launched without melee. Colliding mechs "awkwardly grind against each other" ([PC Gamer, MW5 Mercenaries](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)). Melee arrived in September 2021 as a single button on weapon group 5, only on mechs with hand or lower-arm actuators ([Gematsu](https://gematsu.com/2021/09/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-expansion-legend-of-the-kestrel-lancers-and-free-melee-update-launch-september-23)). Built-in strikes deal **5 / 7.5 / 10 / 12.5 damage from light to assault, doubled with a hand**, on a roughly 4.3-4.8 s cycle at a uniform 17.5 m reach ([Sarna, MW5 Equipment](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/MechWarrior_5:_Mercenaries/Equipment)). The most telling design move came with the Call to Arms DLC. PGI slowed SRM cooldowns from 2.4 s to 2.8-3.4 s to "create room for a full melee strike between SRM cooldown cycles" ([mw5mercs.com](https://mw5mercs.com/news/2022/06/73-call-to-arms-launch-date-intel)), which treats melee as a beat inside a brawler's firing rhythm. What it lacks is any consequence: no stagger, no fall, no kick. Players split between "waste of tonnage" and "absolutely mental", and the AI uses melee "opportunistically rather than well" ([Steam melee discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/784080/discussions/0/4692280570914398205)). MWO removed collision knockdowns in 2013. Players cite abuse, and fear heavies "bowling over" lights ([MWO forum](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/156246-re-implement-knockdown/page__view__findpost__p__3312252)). PGI later admitted that collision code and AI movement are coupled ([MWO December 2016 Roadmap](https://online.mwomercs.com/news/2016/12/1670-december-roadmap-beyond)).

The tabletop kept the interesting part. A kick deals tonnage/5 *and forces the target to roll or fall*, and **a missed kick forces the kicker to roll**. Death From Above costs the attacker leg damage, and a miss is an automatic fall ([Rulespal BattleTech rulebook](https://rulespal.com/battletech-a-game-of-armored-combat/rulebook)). Its damage scales linearly with tonnage (punch t/10, kick t/5, DFA 3t/10) ([MegaMek KickAttackAction](https://github.com/MegaMek/megamek/blob/main/megamek/src/megamek/common/actions/KickAttackAction.java)), which strongly favours assaults. IRONFEUD should scale melee damage sublinearly, or give lights a dodge and initiative edge. HBS BattleTech and AC6 add the "punish window". In HBS, stability damage makes a target Unsteady and then knocks it down, which enables called shots ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/6-battletech-tips)). Weapon "instability" is separate from damage: an AC/20 does 40, a PPC 20, lasers 0, LRMs 2 per missile ([RogueTech NoUseVanillaOverrides](https://github.com/BattletechModders/RogueTech/tree/HEAD/Core/RogueModuleTech/NoUseVanillaOverrides), datamined). AC6's direct-hit multiplier on staggered targets runs 140-205% on sampled weapons ([Fextralife RF-024 TURNER](https://armoredcore6.wiki.fextralife.com/RF-024+TURNER), [SONGBIRDS](https://armoredcore6.wiki.fextralife.com/SONGBIRDS)).

Shipped big-machine games animate these moments as authored states triggered by meters. Titanfall's titans were mocap-heavy ([EA](https://www.ea.com/uk/news/respawn-stryder-ogre-atlas-titans-creation-for-titanfall)), and no major title found ships a fully physics-driven giant walker. IRONFEUD's fully procedural base is unusual, and robotics supplies the right trigger: the **capture point** ([Pratt et al., Humanoids 2006](https://cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-PrattCDG2006.html)). On a linear inverted pendulum, ξ = x_com + v_com/ω with ω = √(g/z). For a centre of mass about 7 m up, ω ≈ 1.18 rad/s, and at 60 km/h ξ sits about 14 m ahead (derived). A running mech therefore always fails the naive "inside the support polygon" test, the same "prototype numbers don't survive genre speeds" trap from CLAUDE.md. The workable criterion is whether ξ, after an impulse, is still reachable by the next foothold within leg reach and swing time. That gives four graded reactions from one continuous quantity. A small hit is only a spring impulse (ankle strategy). A medium hit saturates the gyro torso and throws off aim, while the feet stay planted (hip strategy). A large hit inserts a stumble step (stepping strategy). If ξ escapes the reachable region, the mech falls. Mass, height and leg reach already live in chassis data, so class identity comes for free: heavy mechs move ξ less per hit, and tall ones have more time to step. Leg damage shrinks the reachable region, and a foothold the selector rejects (cliff, rubble) turns terrain into knockdown terrain the AI can reason about. Per-weapon "impact" should be the real momentum transfer, clamped, so cannons and missiles stagger, lasers barely do, and melee does most.

The fall itself belongs in `IronFeud.Logic`, not Godot. Godot's `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` requires a `Skeleton3D`, which IRONFEUD's C#-built rig doesn't have. Community reports also call active ragdolls on Jolt unstable after impacts ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_physicalbonesimulator3d.html), [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/active-ragdolls-in-godot-jolt-is-it-even-possible/117047)). A 2-4 body deterministic mini-sim works better: PD torques toward a "brace" pose, penalty ground contacts, and limb contact targets fed to the existing analytic IK. Get-up becomes 3-5 key contact poses (prone → knee and hand → kneel → stand), solved each tick against raycast ground. That is the Overgrowth "few keyframes plus procedural" approach ([Game Developer, Rosen GDC 2014](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation)), and it keeps "exact IK, cheated inputs" intact. For melee, hitstop gives the weight. Capcom freezes 8/12/16 frames at 60 fps for light/medium/heavy hits ([Capcom SFV column](https://game.capcom.com/cfn/sfv/column/131545)), which is **16-32 ticks at 120 Hz**. IRONFEUD must apply it *locally* to the two participants' limb timelines, because a global time scale breaks headless parity. Strikes should be IK paths in the torso frame with a lunge assist steered through `MechInput`, never position snapping, and should use swept-sphere hits against the existing hit spheres. Whole-body ram momentum m·v makes an assault at 40 km/h hit about 4× harder than a light at 130 km/h, which gives the class difference without special cases (derived). Anti-farm guards are mandatory given MWO's history: diminishing returns on repeated knockdowns, a brief post-get-up stability boost, and AI awareness of its own ξ margin. The tabletop's "a kick risks your own balance" rule becomes emergent, because the stance leg must hold ξ, and it hands lights a high-risk leg-kick job against slow assaults.

## Sensors must trade seeing against being seen, and must never hard-block

MWO set out to make "Information Warfare … about controlling the flow of information on the battlefield" ([MWO Dev Blog 2](https://mwomercs.com/news/2012/01/75-dev-blog-2-information-warfare)). Then it shipped an ECM that, inside a 180 m bubble, denied all missile locks, broke data sharing and made the battlegrid flicker ([MWO Wiki: ECM](https://mwo.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Countermeasure_(ECM))). Even proposed softer versions left it "a *must* for any mech that can bring it" ([MWO forum essay](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/171422-ecm-history-overview-and-revision-an-essay)). The 2015 rework replaced blocks with **a 3 s lock delay and halved the radius to 90 m**. Only the radius cut is listed in the live patch notes ([MWO PTS Phase 2](https://www.mwomercs.com/news/2015/10/1370-mech-rebalance-pts-phase-2), [Patch 1.4.38](https://mwomercs.com/news/2015/11/1420-patch-notes-1438-01dec2015)). IRONFEUD's current ECM, which blocks locks and radar sharing, sits on MWO's original, failed side. MW5 failed the other way: its line-of-sight-only radar dropped contacts the moment you looked away and was called "horrendous" ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/622541-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)). Fans remember the older active/passive radar toggle fondly, but its rules are weakly sourced ([MWO forum](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/154726-activepassive-sensors-and-ecm)).

Other genres solved the information game more cleanly, and their designs transfer. Nebulous: Fleet Command stages intel into packets (contact, class, hull, name, then a status report that goes stale) with ± inaccuracy. Jammers project a cone, so off-axis spotters see through. Jamming leaves a line of bearing back to the jammer. Stacking is capped at about **2.94× the strongest jammer**. Passive ELINT hears an emitter at 1.25× its range ([Nebulous Wiki: Electronic Warfare](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Electronic_Warfare), [Intelligence](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Intelligence), [Radar](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/Radar)). Highfleet compresses this into one rule, "**jamming does not provide stealth**": jamming reveals you to every receiver ([GamePretty EWAR guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radios-warfare-and-ewar/)). Nuclear Option colour-codes its radar warning lines grey (searching), yellow (detected) and red (tracked), and draws a dotted "?" box at a lost contact's last known position ([Nuclear Option Wiki](https://nuclearoption.wiki.gg/wiki/How_detection_works)). Submarine sims add "speed equals noise", with bearing-only passive contacts refined by time and manoeuvre ([ATI Courses: TMA](https://aticourses.com/?p=12281), [Steam: Cold Waters](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/3381662461602302037)).

IRONFEUD's scan model is already richer than MW5's, so the work is reshaping it rather than adding to it. ECM should degrade instead of block: longer lock time, coarser and staler scans, a shorter sharing radius, a visible bearing to the jammer, diminishing returns when stacked, and a cone-shaped "shadow" that a flanking spotter defeats. Contacts should be graded from bearing-only to located, class, model/loadout and status. Each grade costs scan time and ages into a timestamped ghost box instead of vanishing. Signature should become one meter, size × motion × activity: footfall energy (so a running assault is the loudest thing on the map), heat, emissions and the existing fire/jet reveal, shown to the player as a "how loud am I" bar. A passive/active posture revives the toggle with a real reason to use both modes. The HUD needs a grey/yellow/red threat grammar with distinct tones and a "dark again" chirp when the last enemy scan on you lapses. That chirp borrows from MWO's radar-deprivation cue, which players valued ([MWO forum: Radar Dep](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/286293-radar-dep)). Lights get verbs assaults can't buy: a burn-through ping that pierces ECM, gives a 20-30 s snapshot and alerts the target; a two-bearing cross-fix; a decoy that scans can't unmask but eyes can; and a stand-still seismic listen. These also answer the open item "light counterplay at range". The guardrail test: a no-scout lance should lose information-heavy missions more often but still win brawls, and **no EW item should move win rate more than a weapon swap does**.

## Lancemates earn trust through control, legibility and clear lines of fire

The MechWarrior lancemate record is consistent. MW5's AI is "comically inept at understanding the terrain". Allies smash the thing you defend, fire missiles into cliffs, and circle inside their own minimum range ([PC Gamer, MW5 Mercenaries](https://www.pcgamer.com/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review/)). Navigation fixes were still shipping in 2026 ([mw5mercs.com, Chaos Reign Patch 1](https://mw5mercs.com/news/2026/06/191-chaos-reign-patch-1)). Clans answered with control rather than cleverness: F-keys, a radial wheel and an overhead Battle Grid, so you can fix a mate "standing behind a hill doing nothing" ([PC Gamer, MW5 Clans](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/mechwarrior-5-clans-review/)). CGMagazine's main complaint was that "gameplay doesn't stop while you issue commands" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/mechwarrior-5-clans-pc-review)). Players report they mostly use three orders: attack my target, follow me, and guard a position ([Steam MW5 Clans](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2000890/discussions/0/4691153988128083747)).

Shipped squad games converge on three layers. The first is a few one-key intent orders, where Freedom Fighters' "tap sends to the nearest, hold sends to all" is the cleanest form ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/freedom-fighters-review/1900-6075496/)). The second is context orders resolved from what the crosshair points at, as in Republic Commando and SWAT 4 ([Worthplaying SWAT 4](https://www.worthplaying.com/article/2005/5/18/reviews/24695-pc-review-swat-4/)). The third is a group-select layer that scales to hundreds, as in Bannerlord ([Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/troop-commands-in-mount-and-blade-ii-bannerlord)). The best-documented buddy AI, Ellie in The Last of Us, adds the trust rules: stay close and in the player's "room", **never stand in front of the player**, call out only threats the player can then see, and put heroic assists on "really big timers". The authors' summary: "if they ruin what the player is trying to do just once, it's all in vain" ([Dyckhoff, Game AI Pro 2](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)). Halo's Damian Isla frames the ally problem as correctability: "The problem is not that mistakes are made, but there are no ways to correct it" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/in-depth-bungie-on-eight-years-of-i-halo-i-ai)).

For IRONFEUD, an order should become one more input to the existing squad coordinator, not a per-mech override. Orders override doctrine, complete or decay with an acknowledgement bark, and explain refusals ("no shot", "overheating", "leg's gone"). The intent set is small: focus my target, form on me, hold here, flank left or right of my target, scan or spot, fall back, with tap = one and hold = all, and lance-select keys for a company. Doctrine per lancemate maps onto `Personality` and the utility weights: aggression, engagement range, heat discipline, and protect versus hunt. Ellie's balance trick conflicts with IRONFEUD's rules: her hits the player didn't see dealt no damage. That would break hard rule 2 and make watched and headless battles diverge, which would ruin auto-resolve calibration. The rule-compatible alternative is behavioural: lancemates prefer targets the player isn't engaging, take support jobs (marking, flanking for rear armour, breaking a section), and get assist credit in the debrief. Whether that is enough is a design call for Philipp. Fire-team doctrine fits the squad-tactics open item: a heavy or assault as the base of fire, lights as the manoeuvre and spotting element. It also directly gives each class a job. Brothers in Arms is the warning: when every fight is solved by suppress-then-flank, the doctrine becomes a pattern to farm ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_in_Arms:_Road_to_Hill_30)). Enemy lances should therefore use the same order layer, so the matrix can test that no single order pattern wins everywhere. Every trust killer is measurable headlessly and can become a regression test: friendly-fire damage, ticks with a blocked line of fire, distance-to-leader distribution, shutdowns in contact, and order-completion rate. A tactical slow-motion while the command overlay is open is fair under rule 2, because everyone slows equally.

## Missions must make speed and information win conditions, not suggestions

Objective modes give lights a job only when the objective pays and ends the match. MWO's own wiki concedes that "simply destroying the enemy team is always a viable option" in every mode ([MWO Wiki, archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20221119014212/https://wiki.mwomercs.com/index.php?title=Game_Modes)). Conquest players report that the ideal play was "kill 11 and then cap" ([MWO forum](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/193033-conquest-cap-times-still-too-long)). What did make scouts matter was paying for information and small capped modes. World of Tanks pays the spotter for allies' damage ([Wargaming wiki](https://wiki.wargaming.net/en/Credits)). War Thunder's **30 s scouting mark** pays a kill bonus only if the target wasn't already visible ([War Thunder wiki](https://wiki.warthunder.com/mechanics/238-scouting)). MWO's 4v4 Scouting mode had a **50-55 t per-player cap**, and its outcome raised the odds of the follow-up Siege ([MWO tonnage change](https://w.mwomercs.com/news/2016/12/1684-faction-play-tonnage-change-12152016)). In single-player, HBS BattleTech's contract text recommends fast mechs for Target Acquisition, Assassinate, Escort and Recovery ([BattleTech Wiki](https://battletech.fandom.com/wiki/Contracts)), yet a player reports "my mech bay is all assault mechs. Because there is no reason to ever have a light or medium mech" ([Paradox forum](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/battletech-whats-next.1094890/post-24303823)). MW5: Clans funnels the campaign into assaults until the final missions are "a gunnery test" ([Sarna Clans review](https://www.sarna.net/news/mechwarrior-5-clans-review-a-new-narrative-in-mechwarrior/)). Its Wolves of Tukayyid DLC fixed this by opening on farmland "where speed is the main defense", with tonnage passing 300 t only halfway through ([Sarna Wolves review](https://www.sarna.net/news/wolves-of-tukayyid-review-raised-by-wolves/)).

The lesson is that **recommendation text does nothing; forcing functions do**. At IRONFEUD's speeds (36/22/17/11 m/s), a 1.5 km leg takes about **42/68/90/135 s** (derived). A generator can enforce "sum of required travel at class speed fits the clock for lights only", while a hold-for-N objective ensures a pure light lance can't hold the site.

| Template | Class tilt | Forcing function | Counter that keeps it honest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recon / keep a scan on target X for N s | Light | Intel is the win; kills optional | Turrets or an ECM screen on the target |
| Two-site raid under a hard clock | Light, fast medium | Site spacing exceeds what assaults can cover | Each site needs a burst of damage to break |
| Convoy intercept (convoy flees when engaged) | Light, medium | Targets outrun assaults | Escort lance, convoy fires back |
| Hidden objective (found only by scan) | Light then heavy | Heavies can't commit until it is located | Decoys, a relocating objective |
| Hold for N minutes / defend base | Heavy, assault | Endurance, armour, ammo | Second objective or a flanking threat |
| Siege a layered base | Assault + light screen | Fixed defences reward armour | Relay or sensor mast objectives only fast units reach |
| Recovery / extraction | Fast medium, jump heavy | Carrier must run and survive | Carrier focused by enemy fire |
| Attack-and-defend simultaneously | Mixed lance | Lance must split | Both sides scale with the opponent |

Procedural missions need to vary along more axes than the map. XCOM 2's "plot and parcel" keeps hand-built parcels with designer-marked objective and spawn sockets inside procedural plots ([Game Developer, Jake Solomon](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/jake-solomon-explains-the-careful-use-of-randomness-in-i-xcom-2-i-)). That suits IRONFEUD, because objective geometry has to stay tunable against class speeds. Battle Brothers generates contracts from real world state: the bandits actually raid before the contract appears, and the outcome changes the settlement ([Dev Blog #26](https://www.moddb.com/news/dev-blog-26-contracts-and-greenlight-update), [Dev Blog #95](https://www.moddb.com/news/dev-blog-95-contract-changes)). That fits a deterministic campaign sim emitting `BattleSetup`s. MW5's repetition ("kill anything red") came from a small template set and story missions that reused it ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/09/28/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review-legend-of-the-kestrel-lancers/)). XCOM 2's Dark Events warn against stacking random modifiers, so cap mid-mission events per mission. Reinforcements must be telegraphed and placed from campaign state: MW5's spawns "on the front and back of you" were the attrition players modded away ([Sarna, mods](https://www.sarna.net/news/lets-talk-about-mods-for-mechwarrior-5-mercenaries)). Escort missions also need a headless test that the escorted unit completes its route unopposed, because MWO's Escort died on a VIP that got stuck ([MWO forum](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/263389-escort-replacement-concept)). Asymmetric missions should be run in the matrix with attacker and defender compositions as separate cells.

## The campaign must make size cost something every month

The mercenary loop works when damage has an opportunity cost. In HBS BattleTech, "**repair time is more costly than repair money**". Three salvaged parts complete a mech, and one lucky headshot can jump the company a weight class ([PC Gamer BattleTech guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/battletech-guide/)). MW5's soft tonnage cap ties to difficulty and cuts payout if exceeded (players cite up to -95%). Its hubs versus conflict zones change what damage costs depending on where you are ([Steam MW5](https://steamcommunity.com/app/784080/discussions/0/3723946017833861170), [Sarna MW5](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/MechWarrior_5:_Mercenaries)). MW5's launch economy was "busted", with no money pressure for about 15 hours ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/mechwarrior-5-mercenaries-review)). Starsector is the best reference for IRONFEUD's pillar 4. Its per-battle deployment-point cap can be raised by capturing map objectives. Each deployment costs combat readiness, **from about 9% for small hulls to 40% for large ones**, and small ships recover faster. Fleet-wide skills reach full effect only at **≤240 DP** ([Starsector wiki CR](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/CR), [Skills](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Skills), [Objective](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Objective)). Its developer chose soft caps so the player faces "a dynamic choice between having a smaller, elite fleet, and a larger, less organized one" ([Fractal Softworks](https://fractalsoftworks.com/2013/05/25/logistics-fleet-management/)). Battle Brothers limits growth with wages that rise per level, a 12-of-20 field cap, contract difficulty scaled to the strength of the 12 strongest men, and late-game crises that change the world ([BB wiki](https://battlebrothers.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics), [Dev Blog #92](https://www.moddb.com/news/dev-blog-92-late-game-crises)).

Mount & Blade shows both the promise and the failure modes of the long-term vision. Every battle moves several campaign meters at once, and AI lords play by the same rules as the player. But factions snowball, AIs "constantly declare war on each other", and the late game becomes a grind ([Steam Bannerlord](https://steamcommunity.com/app/261550/discussions/0/3415432674396171690)). Vanilla auto-resolve is largely random pairing, where elite troops die to recruits ([Better Auto Calc docs](https://www.nexusmods.com/mountandblade2bannerlord/mods/673?tab=docs)). Starsector's auto-resolve essay states the standard: auto-resolve must use the same stats and counters as real combat, never destroy ships that "would absolutely never, ever get a dent", and not punish skipping a fight ([Fractal Softworks](https://fractalsoftworks.com/2011/11/09/automatically-resolving-combat/)). IRONFEUD can afford what Starsector couldn't in 2011: real headless battles for important fights, plus a cheap mini-sim for galaxy-wide NPC fights, regression-tested against the headless matrix per mission × composition cell. One risk stands out. **If the mini-sim omits spotting, ECM, twist-versus-turn and facing armour, it will quietly reintroduce "biggest is best" in the campaign layer.**

The campaign levers to build, each measurable in deterministic headless runs, are:

1. A per-mission drop cap in tonnage-weighted deployment points, raisable mid-battle by objectives that fast units capture.
2. Per-sortie readiness that assaults spend faster and recover slower.
3. Upkeep and repair bay time that grow super-linearly with tonnage.
4. Dropship transport capacity, with Compact as a logistics quirk.
5. Contract terms that pay for going under the cap.
6. Doctrine bonuses that fade above a lance DP threshold.
7. Difficulty that scales with the strength actually fielded, while world points of interest scale with distance and time.
8. Galaxy crises that need many cheap fast lances in several systems at once.

On the faction layer, use war weariness, minimum war durations and cooldowns, with "wars per faction-year" and "faction count over time" as regression metrics.

## Giant machines sound heavy through recorded material and a protected own-mech mix

Shipped games build weight from physical recordings steered by game parameters. Titanfall 2's sound designers recorded their own sheet metal, anvils and bolts. They contact-miked factory machinery for an "interior-esque heaviness" on footsteps, and drove a real hobby servo from an Arduino, recorded many takes and pitched them down ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/titanfall-2-sound/)). Pacific Rim's team dropped cargo containers on each other at the Port of Long Beach ([HPA](https://hpaonline.com/scott-gershin-takes-the-audience-on-a-sonic-journey/)). Titanfall gave classes distinct voices: the heavy is "guttural" with "growly servos", the agile one "cricket-like" with a whistling "wheeee" dash ([Post Magazine](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2014/March-1-2014/Audio-For-Games.aspx)). Horizon Zero Dawn's machines blend animal and high-tech sources and use "very little metal", partly to avoid listener fatigue ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/08/31/completing-the-experience-3d-audio-and-the-sound-of-horizon-zero-dawn/)). The generic giant-footstep recipe has four parts: a synthesised sine sub at **60-100 Hz**, a 100-200 Hz chest thump, a debris layer that carries scale, and a long tail that fills the gap between slow steps ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/blog/dinosaur-footsteps-sound-effects-made-easy)). IRONFEUD's synthesised footfall covers the sub, the punch and part of the debris. It lacks a recorded "material" body, which is the most likely reason it still falls short. A recorded, low-passed chassis-stress layer and surface-switched debris pools (with a hydraulic settle driven by the existing landing-spring compression) are the high-value additions. Provenance for every sample must be logged.

The mix matters as much as the assets. MW5: Clans' audio lead calls managing low-frequency energy from footsteps, engines, ballistics and lancemates' fire "a ton of effort" ([MW5 Clans audio news](https://mw5mercs.com/news/2025/04/147-flash-storm-frequencies-audio-in-mechwarrior-5-clans)). DICE's HDR audio culls by loudness-as-priority ("Play the right sounds, not all sounds") ([DICE slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/adaptive-mixing-in-frostbite/3128152)). Battlefield 4 then **excluded the player's own foley from HDR** so explosions never cull footsteps ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/heard-about-battlefield-4/)). For a mech game whose identity is footfall weight, that rule is mandatory. A C# HDR-lite over logical loudness can spend the 48-voice pool, with an override so a nearby threat never loses to a distant loud event. In the cockpit camera, own footfalls should *gain* sub and structure-borne thud rather than get quieter, since players complain when cockpit views lose bass ([GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/forum/threads/driveclub-sound-thread.321626/latest)). Servo whine should move from a pure oscillator to parametric playback of recorded sweeps. The analogy is engine audio: joint speed drives pitch or grain position the way RPM does, and load drives a strain layer ([Audiokinetic](https://audiokinetic.com/loop-based-car-engine-design-with-wwise-part-1)). The DSP can live in `IronFeud.Logic/Audio` and feed `push_buffer` from C#, with `get_skips()` in the debug overlay ([Godot docs](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/classes/class_audiostreamgeneratorplayback.rst)).

Weapons need reserved spectral and temporal niches so families stay identifiable when stacked: no "pew pew" lasers, and every weapon readable by threat level ([Post Magazine](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2014/March-1-2014/Audio-For-Games.aspx)). They also need near and far variants, plus incoming-fire cues: a whizz at a wider radius than the crack ([PC Gamer, PUBG](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/how-gunshot-sounds-work-in-playerunknowns-battlegrounds)), and lock warnings that escalate by **beep rate rather than pitch** ([US patent 4182990](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4182990)). The warning voice should follow aviation alerting rules. Critical alerts repeat until resolved, cautions play once, equal-priority voice lines never overlap, a higher priority interrupts, and every alert is paired with a HUD element ([AOPA](https://www.aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2016/july/pilot/warning-systems)). Distinctness from surrounding speech is what research measures. With radio babble in the background, a male voice in a monotone or urgent style was identified fastest ([DRDC](https://www.canada.ca/en/defence-research-development/news/articles/defence-researches-audio-warning-cockpit.html)). The cockpit voice should therefore sound different from lancemate radio in both timbre and processing. Titanfall 2's three synchronised perspective layers (cockpit speaker, exterior PA, helmet radio) are the model for an in-world machine voice ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/titanfall-2-sound/)).

## Copy systems freely, never surfaces

The franchise word marks (BATTLETECH, MECHWARRIOR, BATTLEMECH) belong to The Topps Company, and Microsoft holds the video-game rights ([Wikipedia: BattleTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech), [Justia "mechwarrior"](https://trademarks.justia.com/search?q=mechwarrior)). No registration of the bare word "mech" was found, and it is a genre term used across unrelated titles. The risk sits in franchise compounds and typography ('Mech with the apostrophe, OmniMech) and in proper nouns. Mechanics are free on both sides of the Atlantic: "Copyright does not protect the idea for a game … or the method or methods for playing it" ([US Copyright Office FL-108](https://webharvest.gov/peth04/20041015023438/http://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl108.html)). The CJEU's *SAS v WPL* and German § 2 / § 69a(2) UrhG take the same line ([SCL](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/), [Ferner Alsdorf](https://www.ferner-alsdorf.de/recht-der-entwicklung-von-computerspielen/)). Heat, torso twist, hardpoints, sectioned armour, weight classes, salvage and a mechlab are all free. *Tetris v. Xio* is the warning case: each element was "just a mechanic", but copying all their visual expressions together infringed ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2012/06/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc)). Avoid the record-sheet armour-dot diagram, a specific game's HUD layout, and compiled stat tables.

Litigation in this space has been about **visual design**. In *FASA v. Playmates*, a toy that "bore a strong resemblance" to a franchise mech was still found not substantially similar. The judge called it "just barely outside the reach" of the law after years of litigation ([Sarna: Unseen lawsuits](https://server6.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen_lawsuits)). Harmony Gold sent a cease-and-desist over a single redesigned mech in a 2009 trailer ([Sarna: Unseen](https://www.sarna.net/wiki/Unseen)), so one recognisable silhouette is enough to draw a letter. The practical tests come from the IP notes. Run a "name that mech" test with a 64 px black silhouette shown to a genre fan. Avoid *signature combinations* rather than single features: digitigrade legs, shoulder racks and a canopy are each common, but the specific combination of digitigrade legs, a forward-swept torso, boxy shoulder racks and split arm pods is one famous design. Keep the tiny recessed cockpit, which is distinctive. Avoid skull faces on assaults, bird-legged walkers with missile "ears", forearm cannons paired with a shoulder searchlight, and trash-can bodies. Check every new name against Sarna's chassis list, since many common animal and myth words are taken. Don't use franchise vocabulary as a set (PPC, LRM-20 tube suffixes, Gauss Rifle as an item name, Streak, Artemis, XL engine, Endo Steel, TAG/NARC); IRONFEUD's "particle cannon", "strike rack" and "arc missiles" already avoid this. Don't reproduce the famous boot-up line sequence or its cadence. Keep an asset provenance log covering the licence terms of the Kokoro TTS and Stable Audio Open models, and consult a German IP lawyer before any commercial release. EU design law and UWG imitation protection were not researched and can protect a product's appearance independently of copyright.

## What the code needs before a third body plan

Today `MechChassis` is one mutable class of about 90 fields with no data files. `Create(cls, model)` builds a body (Hauberk defaults or `SicklefootBody()`), applies `Scaled(0.72 / 1.15 / 1.3)` plus class stats, then calls `FitGaitToLegs()`. Heavy and assault are scaled copies of the medium body (`src/IronFeud.Logic/Mech/MechChassis.cs`). The hard-coded assumptions are:

1. Exactly two legs, across the gait table, pose arrays, rig arrays, servo audio and the biped sway and roll formulas.
2. Exactly two arms plus one torso rack, with the fixed section enum `{Torso, ArmLeft, ArmRight, Legs, Cockpit}` (`src/IronFeud.Logic/Mech/Damage/MechDamage.cs`) and three fire bits bound to player controls.
3. One twist ring and five hand-placed hit spheres, with shoulder plates owned by the arms.
4. Nothing scales with size: spring frequencies, the ground-follow rate, angular amplitudes and the ankle limits.
5. `Scale` stands in for size in about 15 places, including gait lift, a collision radius of 3·Scale, the AI blocker sphere, the camera and audio.

Visuals are parametric primitives, with one `Dress*` method of about 100 lines per model in `src/Mech/MechRig.cs`.

The recommended refactor splits the chassis into three records:

- **BodyPlan.** Frame tree, leg list, mount list (frame, offset, section, arc, height), hit-volume list, plate list, cockpit and camera frames, spring parameters, footprint radius, base signature, stability parameters, and dressing as a list of mesh parts.
- **Class stats.** Speed and agility band, heat, structure, sensor range.
- **Loadout.** One weapon per mount.

Store these as JSON in the logic library, with no Godot dependency, plus an optional Godot `Resource` wrapper for editor tuning. That closes the open "chassis `.tres`" item without putting Godot types into logic. Replace the `Scale` proxies with a real size measure (hip height or hit-volume extent). Scale spring frequencies as f ∝ 1/√size, which closes the "spring frequencies don't scale with size" item. Replace the section enum with a per-plan section set. Map the player's three fire bits to *fire groups* over the mount list, so armless or five-mount chassis still play on the same controls. The survey prices new body plans in three tiers. **Cheap** (about a day plus balance runs): a new plantigrade or digitigrade biped with its own proportions and two arm mounts. **Medium**: a reverse knee (another IK root plus a layout value), size-scaled springs, and mount layouts beyond two arms plus one rack (armless, one-armed, torso guns, more than three weapons), all of which need the mount list. **Expensive**: four or more legs (an N-leg gait with per-leg phase, a support-polygon pelvis and per-leg hit volumes), and tanks, which would lose the footfall weight that is the game's identity. Every chassis added this way also needs a silhouette test, a hit-volume budget pinned by a regression test, and a full headless matrix run with both spawn sides and 20+ seeds.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of IRONFEUD's open problems. "Heavies lead kills" and "lights lack counterplay at range" are not tuning problems to be solved inside the current deathmatch modes. They are evidence that the game doesn't yet have the things that employ light units in every successful precedent: objectives with clocks and spread, paid information, EW that is a positional contest rather than an on/off switch, and a campaign where tonnage costs something every month. Buffing lights in a straight fight would push toward parity, which the design rejects. Building the missions and information game gives them jobs, which is the design. The second insight is that IRONFEUD's most distinctive technology, procedural locomotion with springs and footfall impulses, lines up exactly with the genre's most-requested missing feature. Stagger, falls and melee via capture point and momentum transfer would be physical consequences no MechWarrior has shipped, and they arrive with class identity built in, because mass, height and leg reach are already chassis data.

Two cautions run across every section. First, each genre fix that players hated was *invisible*: ghost heat, silent hitbox edits, contacts that vanish without explanation, AI that refuses orders silently. IRONFEUD's debug-first culture is the right antidote, provided the intent overlay becomes a player-facing language for heat holds, lost scans, refusals and the stability margin. Second, the genre's balance history is a record of buffs to the weak beating nerfs to the strong. AC6 1.06 and 1.07, MWO's move from quirks to baselines, and HBS giving missiles a stability role all point the same way. The headless mission × composition matrix is the instrument, and it needs mission types before it can measure pillar 4 at all.

## First-draft slate of original chassis

These are first drafts for discussion, built from generic body-plan families and judged on role, not resemblance. All names are placeholders in Hauberk's armour-and-tool vocabulary. Check each against Sarna's chassis list before adoption, and run each silhouette through the 64 px "name that mech" test. Handling is stated relative to the class band from roadmap item 2. Hauberk stays as the medium all-rounder reference, and Sicklefoot stays a light once it passes the signature-combination check. Build cost refers to the codebase survey's tiers.

### Lights: information, tempo and access

**Coif (scout and electronic warfare).** Body plan: plantigrade biped on very long, thin legs; armless. Silhouette: a slender vertical torso topped by a broad, flat sensor hood wider than the shoulders, so it reads as a "T" on the skyline; legs dark, hood pale. Handling: top light speed, fastest turn, widest twist bounds; lowest stability in the roster, so stumbles come easily. Mounts: one small dorsal mount above the hood that fires over cover the body hides behind, plus two hip pods low on the pelvis. Quirks: (+) longest sensor range and a burn-through ping; (+) high cockpit to peek; (−) the cockpit hit volume is high and exposed; (−) active emissions are heard from farther away. Job: recon objectives, cross-fixes, ECM counterplay. Build cost: **medium** (armless, needs the mount list).

**Bodkin (jumping striker).** Body plan: digitigrade biped with jump jets; an upright, compact drum torso; two thin arms each ending in one long barrel; no shoulder weapons. Silhouette: short torso on tall bent legs, with arms reaching forward past the body. Handling: best air control in the roster; fast twist; moderate speed for a light; small-engine "runs hot" by design. Mounts: one weapon per forearm (free-aiming), one small rack weapon. Quirks: (+) nimble jumper (mid-air heading change); (+) arm mounts converge fast; (−) fragile leg section, so leg damage quickly shrinks its stumble margin; (−) poor heat capacity. Job: flanking for rear armour, jump access to ridge objectives, landing strikes once melee exists. Build cost: **cheap** (digitigrade, two arms plus rack).

**Gadling (hamstring raider).** Body plan: plantigrade on long thin legs, with a hunched torso that is the cockpit pod, carried forward of the hips; two short arms ending in hooked blades. Silhouette: a forward-leaning hunch with a low head, readable as "about to pounce". Handling: fast acceleration, short stopping distance, average top speed for a light. Mounts: one short-range torso weapon (flamer or machine-gun class); blades are melee. Quirks: (+) leg-strike bonus against slower targets (a kick or hook that damages legs and pushes their capture point); (+) compact for transport; (−) a missed strike risks its own balance (the emergent kicker-falls rule); (−) nearly useless at range. Job: hamstringing slow assaults, sabotage objectives, convoy stops. Build cost: **cheap** body, but its job depends on roadmap item 7.

### Mediums: flex, line-holding and base raids

**Pavise (shield brawler).** Body plan: plantigrade humanoid, deliberately lopsided. The left forearm is a broad slab shield with no weapon; the right arm carries the gun. Silhouette: asymmetric, with one wide flat plate on one side and a thinner gun arm on the other; shoulder cover plates. Handling: wide twist bounds (extended twist); good stability for its class; slower arm on the shield side. Mounts: right forearm (one large or two small), torso rack. Quirks: (+) a large shield-arm hit volume with high armour soaks fire when twisted; (+) stable (stagger resistance above its band); (−) the shield-side torso behind the plate is thin if flanked; (−) all firepower on one side, so the right side is a weak point. Job: holding the line for a lance, escorting carriers, pushing into a base with a partner. Build cost: **cheap** (two arms plus rack, one arm unarmed; needs per-arm hit volume sizing, which exists).

**Brigandine (high-shoulder workhorse).** Body plan: plantigrade humanoid with the shoulder joints set *above* the cockpit line and the cockpit recessed low in the chest. Silhouette: a high, level shoulder bar with arms hanging from it and a small chest slot below. Handling: mid-band in everything; average stability. Mounts: forearm weapons that ride high, plus a torso rack. Quirks: (+) overhead arms: its guns clear cover the body hides behind; (−) the low cockpit can't see over that same cover, so firing over walls depends on lance scans or marks (a teamwork tie, not a solo trick); (+) easy to maintain (campaign quirk). Job: the generalist firing line that rewards a scout in the lance. Build cost: **cheap** (proportions only).

**Billhook (reverse-knee peeker).** Body plan: true reverse-knee biped (knees bend backward); armless; the torso is a flattened horizontal wedge hull with a single long barrel along its spine and two small mounts under the hull. Silhouette: a low wedge on backward-bent legs with one long dorsal line. Handling: the highest jumps in the roster (the AC6 3-4× pattern), quick bursts, poor turn rate on the ground. Mounts: dorsal long gun (fixed, torso-aimed), two under-hull short weapons. Quirks: (+) superior jump height for ridge and rooftop access; (−) visible accuracy spread while airborne and just after landing, so it can't become the poptart meta; (−) low stagger resistance and a weak landing gear section. Job: ridge objectives, two-site raids, pressure from angles assaults can't reach. Build cost: **medium** (reverse-knee IK root plus armless mount list).

### Heavies: base of fire with explicit blind spots

**Harrow (squat breacher).** Body plan: plantigrade, wide stance, short thick legs; cockpit recessed between two raised shoulder humps; large hands. Silhouette: low and broad, with the head almost hidden between the humps, which reads as a brawler at range. Handling: slow twist with narrow bounds (well under the heavy band); high stability; strong acceleration for a heavy over short distances. Mounts: one short-range weapon in each forearm above the fist, one rack weapon. Quirks: (+) cover breaker, able to shove through light cover and walls that lights must route around (the "assaults delete cover" scale role, given here to a heavy); (+) heavy melee shove with a strong capture-point push; (−) thin rear armour; (−) short effective range, so lights inside its twist arc are safe. Job: breaching bases, opening paths, brawling at chokepoints. Build cost: **cheap** body; the shove waits for roadmap item 7.

**Gantry (braced long-gun).** Body plan: tall plantigrade biped on long straight legs with narrow hips, carrying a flat "table" torso with one long barrel along one side of it. Silhouette: a crane-like vertical with a long horizontal line on top, asymmetric. Handling: slowest twist of the heavies; a brace stance (crouch, heel spurs down) that raises stability and cuts recoil but locks the legs, and takes a second to enter and leave. Mounts: the long side gun (as the rack, offset to one side) and one small defensive arm weapon. Quirks: (+) braced, its accuracy and stability are the best in the roster; (−) unbraced and moving, it is clumsy; (−) a large side profile (facing armour matters); (−) blind on the side away from the gun. Job: anchoring holds and sieges at range; needs a screen. Build cost: **cheap** if the rack frame can be offset to one shoulder, otherwise **medium**; the brace waits for roadmap item 4.

### Assaults: anchors that cannot be everywhere

**Barbican (top-heavy fortress).** Body plan: plantigrade with stubby legs and very large feet; a very wide chest with a shoulder line well above the head; no face, only a narrow horizontal visor slit under a flat brow plate. Silhouette: a wide upper block on a short base, a horizontal-heavy shape; feet dark, chest pale. Handling: slowest speed and twist in the roster with narrow bounds (about ±60°); very high stability; slow stagger recovery. Mounts: full layout of two arm weapons, two shoulder-top mounts and one torso weapon; first cut uses two arms plus rack. Quirks: (+) protected cockpit (cowled visor); (+) can brace; (−) oversized: easy to hit, can't use narrow passes; (−) logistics: high upkeep, long repair bay time, 1.5× transport space; (−) shortest sensor range. Job: hold-N, siege, defend. A drop cap means it costs most of a lance's budget. Build cost: **cheap** first cut; **medium** for the five-mount layout.

**Trestle (four-legged command platform).** Body plan: quadruped with legs splayed wide like a survey stand and a flat deck body; a central mast-turret that rotates through a full circle, carrying the weapons and the cockpit. Silhouette: low and wide with one vertical mast in the middle, impossible to confuse with any biped. Handling: slow on the ground but steady on slopes; no twist limit thanks to the turret; turns its body slowly; the highest stability in the roster, so near-immune to stagger, and it absorbs recoil. Mounts: two or three turret mounts (long-range or indirect fire), one point-defense mount. Quirks: (+) 360° turret; (+) a command uplink that extends lance scan sharing; (−) a huge side and top profile; (−) per-leg hit volumes mean leg loss is a real threat that lights can hunt; (−) slowest repairs. Job: indirect fire and command hub for sieges and defends. It needs a light screen to see. Build cost: **expensive** (N-leg gait, support-polygon pelvis, per-leg hit volumes). Build it later as a dedicated project.
